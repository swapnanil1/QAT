# Arranging People around Table Example

**Problem:**
In how many ways can 6 people be seated around a circular table such that two specific people always sit together?

(A) 120
(B) 48
(C) 720
(D) 24

## Solution:

This problem involves arranging distinct items (people) in a circle with a constraint (two specific people together). We need to apply the principles of circular permutations and the grouping technique for constraints, as explained in [[15 - Permutations and Combinations/04 - Circular Permutations.md|the circular permutation concept note]].

**Step 1: Identify n and the Constraint**
Total people (n) = 6.
Constraint: Two specific people (let's call them A and B) must always sit together.

**Step 2: Apply the Grouping Technique**
Treat the two specific people (A and B) who must sit together as a single unit or block: (AB).

**Step 3: Determine the Number of Units to Arrange Circularly**
Now consider the units to be arranged around the table:
Unit 1: (AB) - the pair
Unit 2: Person C
Unit 3: Person D
Unit 4: Person E
Unit 5: Person F
We have 5 distinct units to arrange in a circle.

**Step 4: Calculate Circular Permutations of the Units**
The number of ways to arrange `k` distinct units around a circle is `(k - 1)!`.
Here, `k = 5` units.
Ways to arrange the 5 units circularly = `(5 - 1)! = 4!`
`4! = 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 24` ways.

**Step 5: Calculate Internal Arrangements within the Group**
The two people within the grouped unit (A and B) can swap their positions (A sits to B's left, or B sits to A's left). They can be arranged internally in `2!` ways.
Internal arrangements = `2! = 2 * 1 = 2` ways.

**Step 6: Apply the Multiplication Principle**
The total number of ways satisfying the constraint is the product of the ways to arrange the units circularly and the ways to arrange the people within the constrained group.
Total Arrangements = (Circular arrangements of units) * (Internal arrangements)
Total Arrangements = `4! * 2!`
Total Arrangements = 24 * 2
Total Arrangements = 48

**Step 7: State the Final Answer**
There are 48 ways to seat 6 people around a circular table such that two specific people always sit together. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Circular + 'Together' = Grouping + Circular Formula:** For circular arrangements where items must be together, first group the items, then apply the circular permutation formula `(k-1)!` to the *units* (where `k` is the number of units including the group), and finally multiply by the internal arrangement factorial of the grouped items.
2.  **Formula:** Ways = `((n - number_grouped + 1) - 1)! * (number_grouped)!`. Here: `((6 - 2 + 1) - 1)! * 2! = (5 - 1)! * 2! = 4! * 2! = 48`.
3.  **Contrast: No Constraint:** If there were no constraint, the 6 people could be arranged around the table in `(6 - 1)! = 5! = 120` ways (Option A).
4.  **Distinguish from Linear:** A common mistake is to use linear permutation logic. Arranging the 5 units linearly is `5! = 120`. Arranging 6 people linearly with A & B together is `5! * 2! = 120 * 2 = 240`. Remember the `(n-1)!` rule for circles.
5.  **Pitfalls:** Option D (24) incorrectly ignores the internal arrangement (`2!`) of the two people sitting together. Option A (120) ignores the internal arrangement and might also represent the arrangement without constraint. Option C (720) is the linear arrangement of 6 people (`6!`).
