# Calculating Factorials Example

**Problem:**
Evaluate the expression: `(8! * 3!) / (6! * 4!)`

(A) 28
(B) 14
(C) 56
(D) 7

## Solution:

This problem requires simplifying an expression involving division of factorials using the properties defined in [[15 - Permutations and Combinations/01 - Factorial Notation.md|the factorial notation concept]]. The key is to expand the factorials strategically to allow for cancellation.

**Step 1: Identify the Expression**
We need to calculate `(8! * 3!) / (6! * 4!)`.

**Step 2: Expand Factorials for Cancellation**
Expand the larger factorials in the numerator and denominator to match the smaller ones:
*   `8! = 8 * 7 * 6!`
*   `4! = 4 * 3!`

**Step 3: Substitute the Expansions into the Expression**
`( (8 * 7 * 6!) * 3! ) / ( 6! * (4 * 3!) )`

**Step 4: Cancel Common Factorial Terms**
Cancel `6!` from the numerator and denominator.
Cancel `3!` from the numerator and denominator.
The expression simplifies to:
`(8 * 7) / 4`

**Step 5: Calculate the Final Value**
`56 / 4 = 14`

**Step 6: State the Result**
The value of the expression is 14. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Strategic Expansion:** Always expand the larger factorial (`n!`) until you reach the smaller factorial (`k!`) it's being divided by, i.e., `n! = n * (n-1) * ... * (k+1) * k!`. This allows direct cancellation.
2.  **Cancel Systematically:** Cancel identical factorial terms (`n!/n! = 1`) first.
3.  **Simplify Numerical Factors:** After factorial cancellation, simplify the remaining numerical expression (e.g., `(8 * 7) / 4`). You can simplify before multiplying (`8/4 = 2`, so `2 * 7 = 14`) or multiply first (`56 / 4 = 14`).
4.  **Avoid Full Calculation:** Never calculate the full values of large factorials like 8! (40320) or 6! (720) if you can cancel them out. This saves significant time and prevents errors.
5.  **Common Error:** Incorrect cancellation, like trying to cancel `8` with `4` and `6` with `3` individually across the fraction bar before proper expansion, can lead to wrong answers. Stick to cancelling identical factorial terms first. Forgetting to expand `4!` as `4 * 3!` would also lead to errors.
