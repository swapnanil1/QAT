# Arranging People in Seats Example

**Problem:**
In how many ways can 6 boys and 5 girls be arranged in a single row such that no two girls sit together?

(A) 86,400
(B) 1,814,400
(C) 15,120
(D) 39,916,800

## Solution:

This is a permutation problem with a specific constraint ("no two girls sit together"). The standard approach for this type of constraint is the "gap method," utilizing concepts from [[15 - Permutations and Combinations/02 - Permutation (Arrangement) Formula (nPr).md|permutation and arrangement]].

**Step 1: Arrange the Unrestricted Group First**
To ensure no two girls sit together, first arrange the group that *can* sit together, which are the 6 boys.
The 6 distinct boys can be arranged in a row in `6!` ways.
Ways to arrange boys = `6! = 720`.

**Step 2: Identify the Available Gaps for the Restricted Group**
Arranging the 6 boys (B) creates gaps (represented by _) where the girls can be placed to ensure they are separated:
`_ B _ B _ B _ B _ B _ B _`
There are `(number of boys + 1)` potential gaps.
Number of gaps = 6 + 1 = 7 gaps.

**Step 3: Arrange the Restricted Group in the Gaps**
We need to place the 5 distinct girls into these 7 distinct gaps. Since the order in which the girls are placed matters, this is a permutation problem. We need to select 5 gaps out of 7 and arrange the 5 girls in them.
Number of ways to arrange 5 girls in 7 gaps = `7P5`.

**Step 4: Calculate 7P5**
Using the permutation formula `nPr = n! / (n-r)!`:
`7P5 = 7! / (7 - 5)!`
`7P5 = 7! / 2!`
`7P5 = (7 * 6 * 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1) / (2 * 1)`
`7P5 = 7 * 6 * 5 * 4 * 3`
`7P5 = 2520`

**Step 5: Apply the Multiplication Principle**
The total number of arrangements satisfying the constraint is the product of the ways to arrange the boys and the ways to arrange the girls in the gaps.
Total Arrangements = (Ways to arrange boys) * (Ways to arrange girls in gaps)
Total Arrangements = `6! * 7P5`
Total Arrangements = 720 * 2520
Total Arrangements = 1,814,400

**Step 6: State the Final Answer**
There are 1,814,400 ways to arrange 6 boys and 5 girls such that no two girls sit together. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **'Never Together'/'Separated' = Gap Method:** For problems requiring certain items to be separated (like 'no two girls together'), always arrange the *other* group first to create gaps, then place the restricted items into those gaps using permutations (`nPr`).
2.  **Number of Gaps:** The number of gaps created by arranging `k` items in a row is always `k + 1` (including the ends).
3.  **Permutation vs. Combination:** Placing the girls requires permutation (`7P5`) because arranging Girls A, B, C in gaps 1, 2, 3 is different from arranging Girls B, C, A in the same gaps. If we mistakenly used combination (`7C5`), we would get `7C5 = 7! / (5! * 2!) = 21`, leading to `720 * 21 = 15120` (Option C, a common distractor). Order matters here.
4.  **Check n and r:** Ensure you use the correct `n` (number of gaps = 7) and `r` (number of girls = 5) for the `nPr` calculation.
5.  **Contrast: 'All Together':** If the problem asked for all 5 girls to sit together, we would use the grouping method: Treat the 5 girls as one unit `(G_GROUP)`. Arrange the 6 boys and this 1 unit (`6B + 1G_GROUP = 7 units`) in `7!` ways. Then arrange the 5 girls within their group in `5!` ways. Total = `7! * 5! = 5040 * 120 = 604,800`.
