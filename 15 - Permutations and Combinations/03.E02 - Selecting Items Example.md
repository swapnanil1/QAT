# Selecting Items Example

**Problem:**
A cricket team of 11 players is to be selected from a squad of 15 players. How many ways can the selection be made if the captain, who is part of the squad, must always be included in the team?

(A) 1365
(B) 1001
(C) 364
(D) 500

## Solution:

This problem involves selecting a subgroup (team) from a larger set (squad) with a specific inclusion constraint (the captain must be selected). Since the order of selecting players for the team does not matter, this is a combination problem using the `nCr` formula from [[15 - Permutations and Combinations/03 - Combination (Selection) Formula (nCr).md|the combination concept note]].

**Step 1: Identify n, r, and the Constraint**
Total players available (n) = 15.
Number of players to be selected for the team (r) = 11.
Constraint: The captain (1 specific player) must be included.

**Step 2: Handle the Inclusion Constraint**
Since the captain *must* be included, we can consider this player as already selected.
This fulfills 1 spot out of the 11 required spots.
This uses 1 player from the 15 available players.

**Step 3: Determine the Remaining Selection Task**
After including the captain:
Remaining spots to fill on the team = `r - 1 = 11 - 1 = 10`.
Remaining players available to choose from = `n - 1 = 15 - 1 = 14`.

We now need to select 10 players from the remaining 14 available players.

**Step 4: Apply the Combination Formula for the Remaining Selection**
The number of ways to choose 10 players from the remaining 14 is `14C10`.
Using the symmetry property `nCr = nC(n-r)`:
`14C10 = 14C(14 - 10) = 14C4`.

**Step 5: Calculate 14C4**
`14C4 = 14! / (4! * (14 - 4)!)`
`14C4 = 14! / (4! * 10!)`
`14C4 = (14 * 13 * 12 * 11 * 10!) / ((4 * 3 * 2 * 1) * 10!)`
Cancel `10!`:
`14C4 = (14 * 13 * 12 * 11) / (4 * 3 * 2 * 1)`
`14C4 = (14 * 13 * 12 * 11) / 24`
Simplify the calculation: `12 / 24 = 1/2`.
`14C4 = (14 * 13 * 1 * 11) / 2`
`14C4 = 7 * 13 * 11`
`14C4 = 91 * 11`
`14C4 = 1001`

**Step 6: State the Final Answer**
There are 1001 ways to select the team of 11 players such that the captain is always included. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Inclusion Constraint:** If `k` specific items *must* be included when selecting `r` items from `n`, the problem reduces to selecting the remaining `(r-k)` items from the remaining `(n-k)` items. Here, `k=1` (captain), so we selected `(11-1)` from `(15-1)`, i.e., `14C10`.
2.  **Exclusion Constraint:** If `k` specific items must *never* be included, simply remove them from the initial pool and select the required `r` items from the reduced pool of `(n-k)` items. Here, if the captain was excluded, we would select 11 players from the remaining 14, i.e., `14C11 = 14C3 = 364` (Option C).
3.  **Symmetry Rule:** Use `nCr = nC(n-r)` to simplify calculations, especially when `r` is greater than `n/2`. Calculating `14C4` is much easier than `14C10`.
4.  **Combination vs. Permutation:** This is clearly selection (combination) as the order in which players are chosen for the team doesn't matter.
5.  **Distractor Identification:** Option (A) 1365 is `15C11`, the total number of ways to select *any* 11 players without constraint. Option (C) 364 is `14C11` (or `14C3`), the number of ways if the captain is *excluded*. Recognizing these helps confirm the correct logic.
