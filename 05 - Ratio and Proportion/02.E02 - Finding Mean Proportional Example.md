## Problem:

Find the mean proportional between `(3 + sqrt(2))` and `(12 - sqrt(32))`.

(A) `sqrt(28)`
(B) `2*sqrt(7)`
(C) `2*sqrt(11)`
(D) `sqrt(14)`

## Solution:

Let the two quantities be `a = (3 + sqrt(2))` and `b = (12 - sqrt(32))`.
The mean proportional `x` between `a` and `b` is defined such that `a : x :: x : b`.
This gives the formula `x^2 = a * b`, or `x = sqrt(a * b)`.

**1. Simplify the second quantity `b`:**
   `sqrt(32) = sqrt(16 * 2) = sqrt(16) * sqrt(2) = 4*sqrt(2)`
   So, `b = 12 - 4*sqrt(2)`.

**2. Calculate the product `a * b`:**
   `a * b = (3 + sqrt(2)) * (12 - 4*sqrt(2))`
   Use the distributive property (or FOIL method):
   `a * b = 3 * 12 + 3 * (-4*sqrt(2)) + sqrt(2) * 12 + sqrt(2) * (-4*sqrt(2))`
   `a * b = 36 - 12*sqrt(2) + 12*sqrt(2) - 4 * (sqrt(2) * sqrt(2))`
   `a * b = 36 - 12*sqrt(2) + 12*sqrt(2) - 4 * 2`
   `a * b = 36 - 8`
   `a * b = 28`

**3. Find the mean proportional `x`:**
   `x = sqrt(a * b)`
   `x = sqrt(28)`

**4. Simplify the resulting surd:**
   `sqrt(28) = sqrt(4 * 7) = sqrt(4) * sqrt(7) = 2 * sqrt(7)`
   `x = 2*sqrt(7)`

Therefore, the mean proportional is `2*sqrt(7)`.

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Formula:** Remember the mean proportional `x` between `a` and `b` is `x = sqrt(a * b)`.
*   **Simplify First:** When dealing with surds (expressions involving square roots), simplify them as much as possible *before* multiplying or adding. Simplifying `sqrt(32)` to `4*sqrt(2)` made the multiplication easier. See [[12 - Algebra/04 - Indices and Surds.md]].
*   **Careful Multiplication:** Be meticulous when multiplying expressions involving surds. Remember that `sqrt(y) * sqrt(y) = y`. Pay attention to signs.
*   **Simplify Last:** Always simplify the final square root result (`sqrt(28)` to `2*sqrt(7)`) if possible. Look for perfect square factors (like 4 in 28). Check if the options require the simplified form. Option (A) is the unsimplified form, a common distractor.
