## Problem 1:

Two alloys, A and B, contain copper and zinc. Alloy A contains copper and zinc in the ratio 5 : 2. Alloy B contains copper and zinc in the ratio 3 : 4. A third alloy C is formed by mixing alloys A and B. Which of the original alloys, A or B, has a higher concentration (ratio) of copper?

(A) Alloy A
(B) Alloy B
(C) Both have the same concentration
(D) Cannot be determined

## Solution:

**1. Determine the ratio of copper in Alloy A:**
   The ratio of Copper : Zinc in Alloy A is 5 : 2.
   The total parts in Alloy A are 5 + 2 = 7 parts.
   The concentration (ratio) of copper in Alloy A is Copper / Total = 5 / 7.

**2. Determine the ratio of copper in Alloy B:**
   The ratio of Copper : Zinc in Alloy B is 3 : 4.
   The total parts in Alloy B are 3 + 4 = 7 parts.
   The concentration (ratio) of copper in Alloy B is Copper / Total = 3 / 7.

**3. Compare the concentrations (ratios) of copper:**
   We need to compare the ratio 5/7 (Alloy A) with the ratio 3/7 (Alloy B).
   Since both fractions have the same denominator (7), we can directly compare the numerators.
   5 > 3
   Therefore, 5/7 > 3/7.

Alloy A has a higher concentration of copper than Alloy B.

*(Note: The information about mixing to form alloy C is extra information designed to potentially confuse, but it's not needed to determine the relative concentrations of copper in the original alloys A and B.)*
## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Fraction Conversion:** The easiest way to compare ratios `a : b` and `c : d` is often to convert them into fractions representing the proportion of the quantity of interest (like copper in this case). For `a : b`, the proportion of `a` is `a / (a+b)`. For `c : d`, the proportion of `c` is `c / (c+d)`.
*   **Cross-Multiplication:** To compare fractions `p/q` and `r/s`, you can use cross-multiplication. Compare `p*s` and `r*q`.
    *   If `p*s > r*q`, then `p/q > r/s`.
    *   If `p*s < r*q`, then `p/q < r/s`.
    *   In this problem: Compare 5/7 and 3/7. Cross-multiply: 5 * 7 = 35 and 3 * 7 = 21. Since 35 > 21, 5/7 > 3/7.
*   **Common Denominator:** If denominators are easy to make common (as in this case), comparing numerators directly is the fastest method.
*   **Irrelevant Information:** Be wary of extra information in aptitude questions that isn't required to solve the specific question asked. Identify what needs to be calculated and ignore the rest.
## Problem 2:

Anuj and Bimal are evaluating two different investment schemes.
Scheme X has a potential profit to potential loss ratio of 7 : 4.
Scheme Y has a potential profit to potential loss ratio of 11 : 6.
Which scheme offers a higher proportion of potential profit relative to the *total potential outcome* (sum of potential profit and potential loss)?

(A) Scheme X
(B) Scheme Y
(C) Both offer the same proportion
(D) Cannot be determined

## Solution:

**1. Calculate the proportion of potential profit for Scheme X:**
   Ratio of Profit : Loss = 7 : 4
   Total parts = Potential Profit + Potential Loss = 7 + 4 = 11
   Proportion of Profit in Scheme X = Potential Profit / Total Parts = 7 / 11

**2. Calculate the proportion of potential profit for Scheme Y:**
   Ratio of Profit : Loss = 11 : 6
   Total parts = Potential Profit + Potential Loss = 11 + 6 = 17
   Proportion of Profit in Scheme Y = Potential Profit / Total Parts = 11 / 17

**3. Compare the proportions (fractions):**
   We need to compare 7/11 and 11/17.

   *Method 1: Cross-Multiplication*
   Compare `7 * 17` with `11 * 11`.
   `7 * 17 = 119`
   `11 * 11 = 121`
   Since 119 < 121, it means that 7/11 < 11/17.

   *Method 2: Converting to Decimals (Approximate)*
   7/11 ≈ 0.636
   11/17 ≈ 0.647
   Since 0.636 < 0.647, it confirms that 7/11 < 11/17.

Therefore, Scheme Y offers a higher proportion of potential profit relative to the total potential outcome.

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Identify the Goal:** Read carefully to understand what needs to be compared. Here, it's not the direct Profit:Loss ratios (7:4 vs 11:6) but the proportion of Profit relative to the *sum* (Profit + Loss).
*   **Fraction Comparison Techniques:**
    *   **Cross-Multiplication:** For comparing `a/b` and `c/d`, compare `a*d` and `c*b`. This is often the fastest method for two fractions.
    *   **Common Denominator:** Find the LCM of the denominators (`LCM(11, 17) = 187`) and convert fractions (`7/11 = 119/187`, `11/17 = 121/187`). Then compare numerators. This is useful but can involve larger numbers.
    *   **Decimal Conversion:** Useful for quick estimation or when calculators are allowed. Be mindful of rounding errors if precision is needed.
*   **Distractor Awareness:** Note that simply comparing the ratios 7:4 (1.75) and 11:6 (approx 1.83) might lead to the correct answer *in this specific case*, but it doesn't directly answer the question about the *proportion* relative to the total outcome. The question requires comparing 7/(7+4) and 11/(11+6). Always calculate the specific quantity asked for.
