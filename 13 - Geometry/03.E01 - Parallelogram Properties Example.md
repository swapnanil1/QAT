# Parallelogram Properties Example

**Problem:**
In parallelogram PQRS, angle P = `(2x + 50)°` and angle R = `(3x + 20)°`. Find the measure of angle Q.

**(Imagine a parallelogram PQRS, labelled counter-clockwise starting from the bottom left vertex P.)**

(A) 30°
(B) 70°
(C) 110°
(D) 120°

## Solution:

This problem requires using the properties of angles in a parallelogram, specifically the relationship between opposite angles and consecutive angles, as detailed in [[13 - Geometry/03 - Quadrilaterals (Properties).md]].

**Step 1: Identify the Relationship Between Given Angles**
In parallelogram PQRS, Angle P and Angle R are opposite angles.

**Step 2: Apply the Property of Opposite Angles**
In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal.
Therefore, Angle P = Angle R.
Substitute the given expressions:
`2x + 50 = 3x + 20`

**Step 3: Solve the Linear Equation for x**
Rearrange the equation to solve for `x`.
Subtract `2x` from both sides:
`50 = x + 20`
Subtract 20 from both sides:
`50 - 20 = x`
`30 = x`

**Step 4: Calculate the Measure of Angle P (or R)**
Substitute `x = 30` into the expression for Angle P:
Angle P = `2*(30) + 50`
Angle P = 60 + 50
Angle P = 110°

*(Check with Angle R: Angle R = 3*(30) + 20 = 90 + 20 = 110°. Matches, so x=30 is correct).*

**Step 5: Find the Measure of Angle Q**
Angle P and Angle Q are consecutive angles in the parallelogram PQRS. Consecutive angles are supplementary (their sum is 180°).
Angle P + Angle Q = 180°
We found Angle P = 110°.
110° + Angle Q = 180°
Angle Q = 180° - 110°
Angle Q = 70°

The measure of angle Q is 70°. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Identify Relationship:** Correctly identify if the given angles are opposite (equal) or consecutive (supplementary). This is the most crucial step.
2.  **Set up Equation:** Form the equation based on the relationship (`Angle1 = Angle2` or `Angle1 + Angle2 = 180`).
3.  **Solve for x:** Solve the [[12 - Algebra/02 - Linear Equations (One Two Variables).md|linear equation]].
4.  **Find Required Angle:** Substitute `x` back to find one of the angles. Then use either the opposite angle property or the consecutive angle property to find the angle asked for in the question. Double-check which angle is required. In this case, we found P first, then used P+Q=180 to find Q.
5.  **Common Mistakes:** Confusing the properties (treating consecutive as equal, or opposite as supplementary). Solving for `x` and stopping without finding the required angle measure. Calculation errors.
