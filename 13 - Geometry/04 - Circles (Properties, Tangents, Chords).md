# Circles (Properties, Tangents, Chords)

Tags: #quant #geometry #circles #properties #tangents #chords #secants #arcs #concept #formula

---

**Introduction:**

A circle is a fundamental geometric shape defined as the set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a fixed point called the center. Circles possess numerous unique properties related to their chords, tangents, arcs, and angles, which are frequently tested in quantitative aptitude exams.

**Basic Definitions:**

*   **Center (O):** The fixed point from which all points on the circle are equidistant.
*   **Radius (r):** A line segment connecting the center to any point on the circle. The distance itself.
*   **Diameter (d):** A line segment passing through the center with endpoints on the circle. `d = 2r`. It's the longest chord of a circle.
*   **Chord:** A line segment whose endpoints lie on the circle.
*   **Secant:** A line that intersects the circle at two distinct points.
*   **Tangent:** A line that touches the circle at exactly one point (the point of tangency or point of contact).
*   **Arc:** A portion of the circumference of the circle.
    *   Minor Arc: Shorter part. Major Arc: Longer part. Semicircle: Arc formed by a diameter (180°).
*   **Sector:** The region enclosed by two radii and the arc between them.
*   **Segment:** The region enclosed by a chord and the arc it cuts off.
    *   Minor Segment: Area bounded by chord and minor arc. Major Segment: Area bounded by chord and major arc.

**Key Formulas (Mensuration related):**

*   **Circumference (Perimeter):** `C = 2 * pi * r` or `C = pi * d`
*   **Area:** `A = pi * r^2`
*   (Area and Circumference details are primarily covered in [[14 - Mensuration (2D and 3D)/01 - 2D Shapes Area and Perimeter (Triangle, Square, Rectangle, Circle).md]])

**Properties of Chords:**

1.  **Perpendicular from Center:** The perpendicular line drawn from the center of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. Conversely, the line joining the center to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.
2.  **Equal Chords:** Equal chords are equidistant from the center. Conversely, chords equidistant from the center are equal in length.
3.  **Longest Chord:** The diameter is the longest chord in a circle.

**Properties of Arcs and Angles:**

1.  **Angle at Center:** The angle subtended by an arc at the center of the circle is double the angle subtended by the same arc at any point on the remaining part of the circumference.
2.  **Angle in a Semicircle:** The angle subtended by a diameter (or a semicircle) at any point on the circumference is always a right angle (90°).
3.  **Angles in the Same Segment:** Angles subtended by the same arc (or chord) in the same segment of a circle are equal.
4.  **Cyclic Quadrilateral:** A quadrilateral whose vertices all lie on the circumference of a circle.
    *   Sum of opposite angles is 180° (`A+C = 180°`, `B+D = 180°`).
    *   The exterior angle is equal to the interior opposite angle.

**Properties of Tangents:**

1.  **Radius Perpendicularity:** A tangent at any point on a circle is perpendicular (forms a 90° angle) to the radius through the point of contact.
2.  **Tangents from External Point:** The lengths of the two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
3.  **Angle between Tangents:** The line joining the center to the external point bisects the angle between the two tangents from that point.
4.  **Alternate Segment Theorem:** The angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact is equal to the angle in the alternate segment.

**Properties Involving Secants and Tangents (Less Common):**

1.  **Tangent-Secant Theorem:** If a tangent segment and a secant segment are drawn to a circle from an exterior point, then the square of the length of the tangent segment (`PT^2`) is equal to the product of the lengths of the secant segment (`PB`) and its external part (`PA`). `PT^2 = PA * PB`.
2.  **Intersecting Secants Theorem:** If two secant segments are drawn to a circle from an exterior point, the product of the lengths of one secant segment and its external part equals the product of the lengths of the other secant segment and its external part. `PA * PB = PC * PD`.
3.  **Intersecting Chords Theorem:** If two chords intersect inside a circle, the product of the segments of one chord equals the product of the segments of the other chord. `AE * EB = CE * ED`.

**General Approach:**

1.  **Draw Diagram:** If not provided, draw a clear diagram representing the given information. Mark the center, radii, chords, tangents as needed.
2.  **Identify Elements:** Recognize the parts involved (chords, tangents, arcs, angles).
3.  **Apply Relevant Properties:** Select and apply the appropriate circle theorems related to the elements identified. (e.g., use tangent-radius perpendicularity, angle in semicircle, etc.)
4.  **Look for Triangles:** Often, radii, chords, and tangents form triangles (especially right-angled or isosceles triangles - see [[13 - Geometry/02 - Triangles (Properties, Congruence, Similarity).md]]). Apply triangle properties like Pythagoras or angle sum.
5.  **Solve:** Use the properties and derived relationships to solve for unknown angles or lengths, frequently involving [[12 - Algebra/02 - Linear Equations (One Two Variables).md|algebraic equations]].
