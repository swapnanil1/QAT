# Quadrilaterals (Properties)

Tags: #quant #geometry #quadrilaterals #properties #concept #formula #parallelogram #rectangle #square #rhombus #trapezium #kite

---

**Introduction:**

A quadrilateral is a polygon with four sides, four angles, and four vertices. Quadrilaterals are common geometric shapes, and understanding the specific properties of different types (like squares, rectangles, parallelograms, etc.) is vital for solving geometry and mensuration problems in quantitative aptitude. Many problems involve deducing properties based on given information or using properties to find unknown lengths or angles.

**Basic Properties of All Quadrilaterals:**

*   **Four Sides:** They have four line segments as sides.
*   **Four Angles:** They have four interior angles.
*   **Angle Sum Property:** The sum of the four interior angles of any quadrilateral is always 360°.
*   **Two Diagonals:** A quadrilateral has two diagonals, which connect opposite vertices.

**Specific Types of Quadrilaterals and Their Properties:**

1.  **Parallelogram:**
    *   Opposite sides are parallel (`||`).
    *   Opposite sides are equal in length.
    *   Opposite angles are equal.
    *   Consecutive angles are supplementary (sum = 180°). (Related to [[13 - Geometry/01 - Lines and Angles.md|consecutive interior angles between parallel lines]])
    *   Diagonals bisect each other (they cut each other into two equal parts).
    *   Diagonals are generally *not* equal in length and do *not* necessarily bisect the angles.

2.  **Rectangle:** (A special type of parallelogram)
    *   All properties of a parallelogram apply.
    *   All four angles are right angles (90°).
    *   Diagonals are equal in length.
    *   Diagonals bisect each other (inherited from parallelogram).

3.  **Square:** (A special type of rectangle and rhombus)
    *   All properties of a rectangle apply.
    *   All properties of a rhombus apply.
    *   All four sides are equal in length.
    *   All four angles are right angles (90°).
    *   Diagonals are equal in length.
    *   Diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°).
    *   Diagonals bisect the vertex angles (each resulting angle is 45°).

4.  **Rhombus:** (A special type of parallelogram)
    *   All properties of a parallelogram apply.
    *   All four sides are equal in length.
    *   Opposite angles are equal.
    *   Diagonals bisect each other at right angles (90°).
    *   Diagonals bisect the vertex angles.
    *   Diagonals are generally *not* equal in length.

5.  **Trapezium (or Trapezoid):**
    *   Exactly one pair of opposite sides is parallel. These are called the bases. The non-parallel sides are called legs.
    *   Consecutive angles between the parallel sides are supplementary (sum = 180°).
    *   **Isosceles Trapezium:** A trapezium where the non-parallel sides (legs) are equal in length. In this case, base angles are equal, and diagonals are equal.

6.  **Kite:**
    *   Two distinct pairs of adjacent sides are equal in length. (`AB=AD`, `BC=CD`)
    *   One pair of opposite angles (between the unequal sides) is equal. (`∠ABC = ∠ADC`)
    *   One diagonal is the perpendicular bisector of the other diagonal.
    *   One diagonal bisects the angles at the vertices it connects (`∠BAC = ∠DAC`, `∠BCA = ∠DCA`).

**Hierarchy:**
Square < Rectangle < Parallelogram < Quadrilateral
Square < Rhombus < Parallelogram < Quadrilateral

**General Approach:**

1.  **Identify the Type:** Determine the specific type of quadrilateral based on the given properties or diagram.
2.  **Recall Properties:** Access the specific properties (sides, angles, diagonals) associated with that type.
3.  **Break Down (if needed):** Often, drawing a diagonal can divide the quadrilateral into two [[13 - Geometry/02 - Triangles (Properties, Congruence, Similarity).md|triangles]], allowing triangle properties (like Pythagoras, congruence, similarity) to be applied.
4.  **Use Properties:** Apply the relevant properties to set up equations (often [[12 - Algebra/02 - Linear Equations (One Two Variables).md|linear equations]]) or make deductions to find unknown lengths or angles.
5.  **Angle Sum:** Remember the universal 360° angle sum property.
