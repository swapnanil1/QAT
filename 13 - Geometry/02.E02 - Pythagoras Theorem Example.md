# Pythagoras Theorem Example

**Problem:**
In a right-angled triangle, the lengths of the two legs (sides forming the right angle) are 8 cm and 15 cm. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

(A) 17 cm
(B) 19 cm
(C) 23 cm
(D) 20 cm

## Solution:

This problem involves finding the length of the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle given the lengths of the two legs. We can use the Pythagoras Theorem, which states that in a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (`c`) is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides (legs `a` and `b`), as detailed in [[13 - Geometry/02 - Triangles (Properties, Congruence, Similarity).md]].

**Step 1: Identify the Sides**
Let the lengths of the legs be `a` and `b`, and the length of the hypotenuse be `c`.
Given: `a = 8` cm, `b = 15` cm.
We need to find `c`.

**Step 2: State the Pythagoras Theorem**
The theorem states: `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`

**Step 3: Substitute the Known Values**
Substitute `a = 8` and `b = 15` into the formula:
`8^2 + 15^2 = c^2`

**Step 4: Calculate the Squares**
`64 + 225 = c^2`

**Step 5: Sum the Squares**
`289 = c^2`

**Step 6: Solve for the Hypotenuse (c)**
To find `c`, take the square root of both sides:
`c = sqrt(289)`
`c = 17`

The length of the hypotenuse is 17 cm. This matches option (A).

*(Self-check: `8^2 + 15^2 = 64 + 225 = 289`. `17^2 = 289`. The equation holds.)*

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Identify Hypotenuse:** Always correctly identify the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle, which is the longest side). The theorem is `(Leg1)^2 + (Leg2)^2 = (Hypotenuse)^2`. Mixing these up is a common error.
2.  **Pythagorean Triplets:** Recognize common Pythagorean triplets (sets of three integers `a, b, c` such that `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`). Knowing these saves calculation time. Common triplets include:
    *   (3, 4, 5) and its multiples (6, 8, 10), (9, 12, 15), etc.
    *   (5, 12, 13)
    *   **(8, 15, 17)** - This problem uses this triplet! Recognizing it instantly gives the answer 17.
    *   (7, 24, 25)
    *   (9, 40, 41)
    *   (20, 21, 29)
    Learning the basic ones (bolded) is highly beneficial.
3.  **Square Roots:** Be comfortable calculating squares and finding square roots of common numbers. `sqrt(289) = 17` should be known or quickly calculable.
