# Lines and Angles

Tags: #quant #geometry #lines_and_angles #concept #definitions #parallel_lines #transversal #angles #formula

---

**Introduction:**

Lines and angles form the foundational elements of geometry. Understanding their properties and relationships, especially concerning parallel lines intersected by a transversal, is crucial for solving various geometry problems in quantitative aptitude, often forming the basis for deductions in problems involving [[13 - Geometry/02 - Triangles (Properties, Congruence, Similarity).md|Triangles]] and [[13 - Geometry/03 - Quadrilaterals (Properties).md|Quadrilaterals]].

**Basic Definitions:**

*   **Point:** A location in space, having no dimension (length, width, or height).
*   **Line:** A straight path extending infinitely in both directions. It has one dimension (length). Represented by `<--->`.
*   **Line Segment:** A part of a line with two distinct endpoints. Has a finite length. Represented by `-----`.
*   **Ray:** A part of a line starting at a particular point (endpoint) and extending infinitely in one direction. Represented by `----->`.
*   **Angle:** Formed by two rays (sides) sharing a common endpoint (vertex). Measured in degrees (°).

**Types of Angles:**

*   **Acute Angle:** An angle measuring greater than 0° and less than 90°.
*   **Right Angle:** An angle measuring exactly 90°.
*   **Obtuse Angle:** An angle measuring greater than 90° and less than 180°.
*   **Straight Angle:** An angle measuring exactly 180° (forms a straight line).
*   **Reflex Angle:** An angle measuring greater than 180° and less than 360°.
*   **Complete Angle:** An angle measuring exactly 360°.

**Angle Relationships:**

*   **Complementary Angles:** Two angles whose sum is 90°.
*   **Supplementary Angles:** Two angles whose sum is 180°.
*   **Adjacent Angles:** Two angles that share a common vertex and a common side but do not overlap.
*   **Linear Pair:** Two adjacent angles whose non-common sides form a straight line. Their sum is always 180° (they are supplementary).
*   **Vertically Opposite Angles:** Angles formed by the intersection of two lines. They are opposite to each other and are always equal.

**Parallel Lines and Transversals:**

*   **Parallel Lines:** Two lines in a plane that never intersect, no matter how far they are extended. They maintain a constant distance apart. Notation: `l || m`.
*   **Transversal:** A line that intersects two or more other lines (often parallel lines) at distinct points.

When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, specific pairs of angles are formed with important relationships:

Let the two parallel lines be `l` and `m`, and the transversal be `t`.

1.  **Corresponding Angles:** Angles in the same relative position at each intersection. They are **equal**. (e.g., top-left angle at the first intersection = top-left angle at the second intersection).
2.  **Alternate Interior Angles:** Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and between the parallel lines. They are **equal**.
3.  **Alternate Exterior Angles:** Angles on opposite sides of the transversal and outside the parallel lines. They are **equal**.
4.  **Consecutive Interior Angles (or Co-interior Angles):** Angles on the same side of the transversal and between the parallel lines. They are **supplementary** (sum = 180°).
5.  **Vertically Opposite Angles:** Angles opposite each other at the same intersection point. They are **equal**.
6.  **Linear Pairs:** Adjacent angles on a straight line. They are **supplementary**.

**Key Properties Summary (for Parallel Lines):**

*   Corresponding Angles are Equal.
*   Alternate Interior Angles are Equal.
*   Alternate Exterior Angles are Equal.
*   Consecutive Interior Angles are Supplementary (Sum = 180°).
*   Conversely, if any of these conditions hold true, then the two lines intersected by the transversal must be parallel.

**General Approach:**

1.  **Identify:** Recognize the geometric setup (intersecting lines, parallel lines, transversal).
2.  **Apply Definitions:** Use the definitions of angle types and relationships.
3.  **Use Parallel Line Properties:** If lines are parallel, apply the angle equalities or supplementary relationships.
4.  **Deduce:** Combine known angles and relationships to find unknown angles. Often involves setting up simple [[12 - Algebra/02 - Linear Equations (One Two Variables).md|Linear Equations]].
