# Tangent Properties Example

**Problem:**
Point P is 13 cm away from the center O of a circle. A tangent line is drawn from point P, touching the circle at point T. If the radius of the circle is 5 cm, what is the length of the tangent segment PT?

**(Imagine a circle with center O. Point P is outside the circle. A line segment connects O to P. Another line segment goes from P and touches the circle at point T (tangent). A radius OT connects the center to the point of tangency T.)**

(A) 8 cm
(B) 10 cm
(C) 12 cm
(D) sqrt(194) cm

## Solution:

This problem involves using the fundamental property of a tangent to a circle: it is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. This forms a right-angled triangle, allowing us to use the Pythagoras theorem. Refer to [[13 - Geometry/04 - Circles (Properties, Tangents, Chords).md]] for tangent properties.

**Step 1: Identify the Geometric Setup**
We have:
*   Center of the circle: O
*   External point: P
*   Point of tangency: T
*   Distance from center to external point: OP = 13 cm
*   Radius of the circle: OT = 5 cm
*   Tangent segment: PT (length to be found)

**Step 2: Apply the Tangent-Radius Perpendicularity Property**
The radius OT is perpendicular to the tangent PT at the point of contact T.
Therefore, Angle OTP = 90°.
This means that triangle OTP is a right-angled triangle with the right angle at T.

**Step 3: Identify Sides of the Right-angled Triangle OTP**
*   The side opposite the right angle (Angle T) is the hypotenuse: OP = 13 cm.
*   The other two sides (legs) are the radius OT = 5 cm and the tangent segment PT.

**Step 4: Apply the Pythagoras Theorem**
According to Pythagoras theorem ([[13 - Geometry/02 - Triangles (Properties, Congruence, Similarity).md]]):
`(Leg1)^2 + (Leg2)^2 = (Hypotenuse)^2`
`(OT)^2 + (PT)^2 = (OP)^2`

**Step 5: Substitute Known Values and Solve for PT**
`5^2 + (PT)^2 = 13^2`
`25 + (PT)^2 = 169`

Subtract 25 from both sides:
`(PT)^2 = 169 - 25`
`(PT)^2 = 144`

Take the square root of both sides:
`PT = sqrt(144)`
`PT = 12` cm

The length of the tangent segment PT is 12 cm. This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Key Property:** The crucial step is recognizing that the tangent (PT) and the radius (OT) form a right angle at the point of tangency (T). This allows the formation of the right-angled triangle OTP.
2.  **Pythagoras Application:** Once the right triangle is identified, apply the Pythagoras theorem: `(Radius)^2 + (Tangent Length)^2 = (Distance from Center to External Point)^2`.
3.  **Identify Hypotenuse:** The hypotenuse is *always* the line segment connecting the center (O) to the external point (P), as it's opposite the right angle at T. Do not mistake the tangent length for the hypotenuse.
4.  **Pythagorean Triplets:** Notice that (5, 12, 13) is a common Pythagorean triplet (like (3,4,5) and (8,15,17)). Recognizing this triplet when you see the radius (5) and the distance (13) instantly tells you the tangent length must be 12, saving calculation time.
