# Angle in Semicircle Example

**Problem:**
In the figure below, O is the center of the circle and AB is the diameter. Point C is on the circumference. If angle CAB = 35°, find the measure of angle ABC.

**(Imagine a circle with center O. Diameter AB is drawn horizontally. Point C is on the upper circumference, forming triangle ABC.)**

(A) 35°
(B) 45°
(C) 55°
(D) 90°

## Solution:

This problem utilizes the properties of angles within a circle, specifically the angle subtended by a diameter, as discussed in [[13 - Geometry/04 - Circles (Properties, Tangents, Chords).md]].

**Step 1: Identify the Key Geometric Feature**
Triangle ABC is inscribed in the circle such that one of its sides, AB, is the diameter of the circle.

**Step 2: Apply the Angle in a Semicircle Theorem**
The theorem states that the angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the circumference is a right angle (90°).
Therefore, the angle at vertex C, which is angle ACB, must be 90°.
So, Angle ACB = 90°.

**Step 3: Consider Triangle ABC**
Now we have a triangle ABC with the following angles:
*   Angle CAB (or Angle A) = 35° (Given)
*   Angle ACB (or Angle C) = 90° (Angle in a semicircle)
*   Angle ABC (or Angle B) = ? (To be found)

**Step 4: Apply the Angle Sum Property of Triangles**
The sum of angles in any triangle is 180° (from [[13 - Geometry/02 - Triangles (Properties, Congruence, Similarity).md]]).
Angle CAB + Angle ABC + Angle ACB = 180°
35° + Angle ABC + 90° = 180°

**Step 5: Solve for Angle ABC**
Combine the known angles:
Angle ABC + 125° = 180°
Subtract 125° from both sides:
Angle ABC = 180° - 125°
Angle ABC = 55°

The measure of angle ABC is 55°. This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Spot the Diameter:** Recognize immediately when a triangle is inscribed with one side being the diameter. This instantly tells you the angle opposite the diameter is 90°.
2.  **Right Triangle Focus:** The problem then becomes finding an angle in a right-angled triangle when another acute angle is known.
3.  **Complementary Angles:** In a right-angled triangle, the two acute angles are complementary (they add up to 90°). Once you know Angle ACB = 90°, you can directly calculate the other acute angle: Angle ABC = 90° - Angle CAB = 90° - 35° = 55°. This avoids using the full 180° sum property and is slightly faster.
4.  **Common Distractor:** Option D (90°) is a distractor representing the angle at C, not the requested angle at B. Always check which angle is being asked for.
