# Surface Area Volume of Sphere Example

**Problem:**
The volume of a sphere is 38808 cm³. Find its surface area. (Use `pi = 22/7`)

(A) 5544 cm²
(B) 2772 cm²
(C) 1386 cm²
(D) 4158 cm²

## Solution:

This problem requires finding the radius (`r`) of the sphere from its given volume and then using that radius to calculate the surface area. The formulas for sphere volume and surface area are detailed in [[14 - Mensuration (2D and 3D)/02 - 3D Shapes Surface Area and Volume (Cube, Cuboid, Cylinder, Cone, Sphere).md|the concept note on 3D shapes]].

**Step 1: Identify Given Information and Goal**
Given: Volume (V) = 38808 cm³. Use `pi = 22/7`.
Goal: Find the Surface Area (SA) of the sphere.

**Step 2: Use the Volume Formula to Find the Radius (r)**
The formula for the volume of a sphere is `V = (4/3) * pi * r^3`.
Substitute the given values:
38808 = (4/3) * (22/7) * `r^3`
38808 = (88/21) * `r^3`

**Step 3: Solve for r³**
Rearrange the equation to isolate `r^3`:
`r^3 = 38808 * (21 / 88)`
Divide 38808 by 88. (Hint: Both are divisible by 8. 38808 / 8 = 4851. 88 / 8 = 11. Now divide 4851 by 11. 4851 / 11 = 441).
So, 38808 / 88 = 441.
`r^3 = 441 * 21`
We know `441 = 21 * 21`.
So, `r^3 = 21 * 21 * 21`
`r^3 = 21^3`

**Step 4: Find the Radius (r)**
Take the cube root of both sides:
`r = 21` cm

**Step 5: Use the Radius to Calculate the Surface Area (SA)**
The formula for the surface area of a sphere is `SA = 4 * pi * r^2`.
Substitute the value of `r = 21` and `pi = 22/7`:
`SA = 4 * (22/7) * (21)^2`
`SA = 4 * (22/7) * 21 * 21`
Cancel the 7 in the denominator with one 21: `21 / 7 = 3`.
`SA = 4 * 22 * 3 * 21`
`SA = 88 * 63`
Calculate the product: 88 * 63 = 88 * (60 + 3) = 5280 + 264 = 5544.
`SA = 5544` cm²

**Step 6: State the Final Surface Area**
The surface area of the sphere is 5544 cm². This matches option (A).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Relationship via Radius:** Both Volume (`(4/3) * pi * r^3`) and Surface Area (`4 * pi * r^2`) of a sphere depend only on the radius (`r`). Problems often give one to find the other.
2.  **Isolating r³:** Be careful when rearranging the volume formula: `r^3 = V * (3 / (4 * pi))`. Ensure you multiply by the reciprocal of `(4/3) * pi`.
3.  **Cube Roots:** Finding cube roots of large numbers like `r^3 = 9261` (which is `441 * 21`) can be tricky. Look for factors that are perfect cubes, or use prime factorization. Alternatively, estimate: `20^3 = 8000`, `30^3 = 27000`. Since 9261 ends in 1, its cube root must end in 1 (as only `1^3=1`, `...`). So, try 21. `21 * 21 * 21 = 441 * 21 = 9261`. This estimation helps significantly.
4.  **Formula Accuracy:** Ensure you use the correct formulas (`(4/3)pi*r^3` for V, `4*pi*r^2` for SA). Common mistakes involve mixing up `r^2` and `r^3`, or forgetting the `(4/3)` factor in volume.
5.  **Distractor Analysis:** Option (C) 1386 cm² is `pi * r^2`, the area of a great circle. Option (B) 2772 cm² is `2 * pi * r^2`, the curved surface area of a hemisphere. Option (D) 4158 cm² is `3 * pi * r^2`, the total surface area of a hemisphere. These are based on related but incorrect formulas.
6.  **Calculation Simplification:** Perform cancellations (`21/7 = 3`) early in the SA calculation to work with smaller numbers.
