# Example: Population Growth (Finding Past Population)

Tags: #quant #percentages #applications #population_growth #successive_change #example #tricky_question

Parent Concept: [[02 - Percentages/04 - Applications (Population, Depreciation)]]

## Problem

The population of a town increased at a rate of 10% per annum for two consecutive years. If the population after these two years is 48,400, what was the population at the beginning of the first year?

## Analysis

This problem provides the final population after two successive percentage increases and asks for the initial population. We can use the population growth formula and rearrange it to find the initial value (`P0`).

Let:
*   `P0` = Initial Population (at the beginning of the first year) = ?
*   `R` = Annual rate of increase = 10%
*   `n` = Number of years = 2
*   `P_n` = Population after `n` years (`P_2` in this case) = 48,400

We will use the formula: `P0 = P_n / (1 + R/100)^n`

## Solution

1.  **Identify the values:**
    *   `P_n = 48,400`
    *   `R = 10`
    *   `n = 2`

2.  **Calculate the growth factor (1 + R/100):**
    *   Growth Factor = `1 + 10/100 = 1 + 0.10 = 1.1`

3.  **Apply the formula to find P0:**
    *   `P0 = P_n / (Growth Factor)^n`
    *   `P0 = 48,400 / (1.1)^2`

4.  **Calculate the denominator:**
    *   `(1.1)^2 = 1.1 * 1.1 = 1.21`

5.  **Calculate P0:**
    *   `P0 = 48,400 / 1.21`
    *   To simplify the division, multiply numerator and denominator by 100 to remove the decimal:
        `P0 = 4,840,000 / 121`
    *   Recognize that 121 is 11 * 11. Check if 484 is divisible by 11 (4-8+4 = 0, yes) or 121.
    *   `484 / 121 = 4`
    *   Therefore, `4,840,000 / 121 = 40,000`

## Alternative Method (Step-by-Step Reverse)

Let `P1` be the population after the first year.
The population after the second year (`P_2`) is `P1` increased by 10%:
`P_2 = P1 * (1 + 10/100) = P1 * 1.1`
`48,400 = P1 * 1.1`
`P1 = 48,400 / 1.1 = 484,000 / 11 = 44,000`

Now, the population after the first year (`P1`) was the initial population (`P0`) increased by 10%:
`P1 = P0 * (1 + 10/100) = P0 * 1.1`
`44,000 = P0 * 1.1`
`P0 = 44,000 / 1.1 = 440,000 / 11 = 40,000`

## Shortcut/Tip

*   When working backwards from a final value after `n` successive percentage changes, divide the final value by the multiplication factor `n` times (or divide by the factor raised to the power of `n`).
*   Recognizing squares (like 1.1² = 1.21 or 11² = 121) can speed up calculations.

**Answer:** The population at the beginning of the first year was 40,000.
