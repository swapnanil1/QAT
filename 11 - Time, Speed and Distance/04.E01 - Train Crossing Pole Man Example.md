# Train Crossing Pole/Man Example

**Problem:**

A train 280 meters long is running at a speed of 63 km/hr. How long will it take the train to cross a stationary electric pole?

(A) 12 seconds
(B) 15 seconds
(C) 16 seconds
(D) 18 seconds

## Solution:

This scenario involves a train crossing a stationary object of negligible length (an electric pole). As described in [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/04 - Problems on Trains.md]], the distance the train needs to cover to completely pass the pole is equal to its own length.

**Step 1: Identify Given Information**
Length of the train (`Lt`) = 280 meters.
Speed of the train (`St`) = 63 km/hr.
Object: Stationary electric pole (negligible length).

**Step 2: Determine the Distance to be Covered**
Distance (D) = Length of the train = `Lt` = 280 meters.

**Step 3: Ensure Consistent Units**
The distance is in meters, but the speed is in km/hr. We need to convert the speed to m/s to calculate the time in seconds.
Conversion factor: `1 km/hr = 5/18 m/s`.
`St` (in m/s) = `63 * (5/18)`

Simplify the calculation: Both 63 and 18 are divisible by 9.
`St` (in m/s) = `(63/9) * (5 / (18/9))`
`St` (in m/s) = `7 * (5 / 2)`
`St` (in m/s) = `35 / 2` = 17.5 m/s.

**Step 4: Calculate the Time Taken**
Use the formula `Time = Distance / Speed`.
Time (`T`) = `D / St`
`T` = 280 meters / (35/2 m/s)
`T` = 280 * (2/35) seconds

Simplify the calculation: 280 is divisible by 35 (280 / 35 = 8, since 35*2=70 and 70*4=280).
`T` = 8 * 2 seconds
`T` = 16 seconds.

The train will take 16 seconds to cross the electric pole. This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip

1.  **Recognize Scenario:** Train crossing a point object (pole, man). Distance = Length of Train.
2.  **Convert Speed:** Convert speed from km/hr to m/s (`* 5/18`). Performing `63 * 5 / 18` quickly is key. Simplification `(7*9*5)/(2*9) = 35/2` helps.
3.  **Calculate Time:** `Time = Length / Speed (m/s)`. `T = 280 / (35/2) = 280 * 2 / 35 = 8 * 2 = 16` seconds.

Always perform the unit conversion for speed before calculating the time. A common error is dividing distance in meters by speed in km/hr directly.
