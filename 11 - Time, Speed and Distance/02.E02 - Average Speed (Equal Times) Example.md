# Average Speed (Equal Times) Example

**Problem:**

A car travels for 2 hours at a speed of 40 km/hr and then travels for another 2 hours at a speed of 60 km/hr. What is the average speed of the car for the entire 4-hour journey?

(A) 48 km/hr
(B) 50 km/hr
(C) 52 km/hr
(D) 45 km/hr

## Solution:

This problem involves a journey divided into two segments where the **time duration** of each segment is the same (2 hours), but the speeds are different. As discussed in [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/02 - Average Speed Concepts.md]], when time intervals are equal, the average speed is the simple arithmetic mean of the speeds.

**Method 1: Using the Arithmetic Mean Formula**

Since the time spent travelling at each speed is equal (2 hours each), the average speed is the simple average (arithmetic mean) of the individual speeds.
Speed 1 (s1) = 40 km/hr
Speed 2 (s2) = 60 km/hr
Time interval 1 (t1) = 2 hours
Time interval 2 (t2) = 2 hours (t1 = t2)

Average Speed = `(s1 + s2) / 2`
Average Speed = `(40 + 60) / 2`
Average Speed = `100 / 2`
Average Speed = 50 km/hr.

**Method 2: Using Basic Definitions (Total Distance / Total Time)**

1.  Calculate the distance covered in each segment:
    Distance 1 (`d1`) = Speed 1 * Time 1 = 40 km/hr * 2 hr = 80 km.
    Distance 2 (`d2`) = Speed 2 * Time 2 = 60 km/hr * 2 hr = 120 km.

2.  Calculate the Total Distance:
    Total Distance = `d1 + d2` = 80 km + 120 km = 200 km.

3.  Calculate the Total Time:
    Total Time = Time 1 + Time 2 = 2 hours + 2 hours = 4 hours.

4.  Calculate the Average Speed:
    Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time
    Average Speed = 200 km / 4 hours
    Average Speed = 50 km/hr.

Both methods yield the same result: 50 km/hr. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip

1.  **Recognize Equal Times:** The key is identifying that the *time intervals* spent at different speeds are equal (2 hours each).
2.  **Apply Arithmetic Mean:** When times are equal, the average speed is simply the average of the speeds: `(Speed1 + Speed2 + ... + SpeedN) / N`. For two speeds: `(s1 + s2) / 2`.
3.  **Avoid Harmonic Mean:** Do *not* use the harmonic mean formula `(2*s1*s2)/(s1+s2)` here. That formula `(2*40*60)/(40+60) = 4800/100 = 48` km/hr (Option A) is only valid when *distances* are equal. Because more distance is covered at the higher speed (60 km/hr), the average speed should be exactly in the middle of the two speeds.
