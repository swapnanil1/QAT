# km/hr to m/s Conversion Example

**Problem:**

A train travels at a speed of 72 km/hr. How many seconds will it take to cover a distance of 400 meters?

(A) 15 seconds
(B) 18 seconds
(C) 20 seconds
(D) 25 seconds

## Solution:

This problem requires calculating the time taken, given distance and speed. However, the units are inconsistent: speed is in km/hr, and distance is in meters. We need to convert the speed to m/s first, using the conversion factor from [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/01 - Basic Formula (D=S T) and Unit Conversions.md]].

**Step 1: Convert Speed from km/hr to m/s**
Speed (km/hr) = 72 km/hr.
Conversion factor: `1 km/hr = 5/18 m/s`.
Speed (m/s) = `Speed (km/hr) * (5/18)`
Speed (m/s) = `72 * (5/18)`

Simplify the calculation: 72 is divisible by 18 (72 / 18 = 4).
Speed (m/s) = `4 * 5 = 20` m/s.

**Step 2: Calculate Time Taken**
Now we have:
Speed (S) = 20 m/s
Distance (D) = 400 m
We need to find Time (T).

Using the formula `Time = Distance / Speed`:
`T = D / S`
`T = 400 m / 20 m/s`
`T = 40 / 2 = 20` seconds.

The train will take 20 seconds to cover a distance of 400 meters. This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip

1.  **Identify Unit Mismatch:** Recognize immediately that speed (km/hr) and distance (m) units don't match for a direct calculation of time in seconds.
2.  **Perform Conversion:** Convert km/hr to m/s using the `* 5/18` factor. It's useful to remember common multiples: 18 km/hr = 5 m/s, 36 km/hr = 10 m/s, 54 km/hr = 15 m/s, 72 km/hr = 20 m/s, 90 km/hr = 25 m/s, 108 km/hr = 30 m/s. Recognizing 72 km/hr directly as 20 m/s saves calculation time.
3.  **Apply Formula:** Use `T = D / S` with consistent units. `T = 400 / 20 = 20` seconds.

Avoid calculating with inconsistent units (like 400 / 72), which would give a meaningless result. Always convert first.
