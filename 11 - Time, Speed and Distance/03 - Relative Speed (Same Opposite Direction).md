# Relative Speed (Same/Opposite Direction)

**Concept:**

Relative speed is the speed of an object with respect to another object. It's a crucial concept when dealing with problems involving two or more moving objects, such as trains approaching each other, cars overtaking, or boats in streams (though boats have a specific variation covered in [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/05 - Boats and Streams.md]]).

The core idea is to analyze the motion from the perspective of one of the objects, effectively treating that object as stationary and calculating the speed at which the other object is approaching or receding from it.

**Calculating Relative Speed:**

The calculation depends on the direction of motion of the objects relative to each other.

1.  **Objects Moving in Opposite Directions:**
    *   When two objects are moving towards each other or away from each other (i.e., in opposite directions), their relative speed is the **sum** of their individual speeds.
    *   Imagine two cars approaching each other. From the perspective of one car, the other car appears to be coming closer at a speed that combines both their speeds. The distance between them decreases rapidly.
    *   Let the speeds of the two objects be `S1` and `S2`.
    *   Relative Speed (`S_rel`) = `S1 + S2`

2.  **Objects Moving in the Same Direction:**
    *   When two objects are moving in the same direction (e.g., one chasing or overtaking the other), their relative speed is the **difference** between their individual speeds.
    *   Imagine a faster car overtaking a slower car. From the perspective of the slower car, the faster car is gaining on it at a speed equal to the difference between their speeds. The distance between them decreases by this difference each unit of time.
    *   Let the speeds of the two objects be `S1` and `S2` (where `S1` is usually considered the faster speed).
    *   Relative Speed (`S_rel`) = `|S1 - S2|` (Absolute difference, as speed is positive). Usually written as `S_faster - S_slower`.

**Applying Relative Speed:**

Relative speed simplifies problems involving multiple moving objects by allowing us to calculate the **time taken for the distance between them to change** (e.g., time to meet, time to overtake).

*   **Time = Distance / Relative Speed**

Where:
*   **Time:** Time taken to meet, cross, or for the distance between them to change by a certain amount.
*   **Distance:** The initial distance between the objects, or the length that needs to be covered relative to each other (e.g., the sum of lengths of two trains when crossing).
*   **Relative Speed:** Calculated based on whether they are moving in the same or opposite directions.

**Common Applications:**

*   **Meeting Point:** Finding the time or location where two objects starting from different points and moving towards each other will meet. Use `S_rel = S1 + S2`.
*   **Overtaking/Crossing:** Finding the time taken for a faster object to overtake or completely pass a slower object moving in the same direction. Use `S_rel = S_faster - S_slower`. (See [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/04 - Problems on Trains.md]]).
*   **Crossing Each Other (Opposite Directions):** Finding the time taken for two objects (like trains) moving towards each other to completely pass each other. Use `S_rel = S1 + S2`. (See [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/04 - Problems on Trains.md]]).

**Example Scenarios:**

*   Car A (60 km/hr) and Car B (40 km/hr) move towards each other. Relative Speed = 60 + 40 = 100 km/hr.
*   Car A (60 km/hr) chases Car B (40 km/hr) moving in the same direction. Relative Speed = 60 - 40 = 20 km/hr.
