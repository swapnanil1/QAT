# Finding Downstream Upstream Speed Example

**Problem:**
A man can row a boat at a speed of 10 km/hr in still water. The speed of the stream is 2 km/hr. How much total time will he take to go to a place 48 km away and return to the starting point?

(A) 8 hours
(B) 9.6 hours
(C) 10 hours
(D) 12 hours

## Solution:

This problem requires calculating the time taken for the downstream journey and the upstream journey separately and then summing them up. We first need to determine the effective speeds for each direction using the concepts from [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/05 - Boats and Streams.md]].

**Step 1: Identify Given Information**
Speed of boat in still water (`B`) = 10 km/hr.
Speed of stream (`S`) = 2 km/hr.
Distance one way (`Dist`) = 48 km.

**Step 2: Calculate Downstream Speed (D)**
When going downstream (with the current), the effective speed is the sum of the boat's speed and the stream's speed.
`D = B + S`
`D` = 10 km/hr + 2 km/hr = 12 km/hr.

**Step 3: Calculate Upstream Speed (U)**
When returning upstream (against the current), the effective speed is the difference between the boat's speed and the stream's speed.
`U = B - S`
`U` = 10 km/hr - 2 km/hr = 8 km/hr.

**Step 4: Calculate Time Taken for Downstream Journey (Td)**
Use the formula `Time = Distance / Speed` with the downstream speed.
`Td = Dist / D`
`Td` = 48 km / 12 km/hr = 4 hours.

**Step 5: Calculate Time Taken for Upstream Journey (Tu)**
Use the formula `Time = Distance / Speed` with the upstream speed.
`Tu = Dist / U`
`Tu` = 48 km / 8 km/hr = 6 hours.

**Step 6: Calculate Total Time**
Total time = Time Downstream + Time Upstream
Total Time = `Td + Tu`
Total Time = 4 hours + 6 hours = 10 hours.

The total time taken for the round trip is 10 hours.

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Identify Speeds:** Quickly determine `B` and `S`.
2.  **Calculate D & U:** Find `D = B + S` and `U = B - S`.
3.  **Calculate Times:** Calculate `Td = Dist / D` and `Tu = Dist / U`.
4.  **Sum Times:** Add `Td` and `Tu`.

**Common Pitfall:** Avoid calculating an average speed for the whole journey and dividing the total distance (48 + 48 = 96 km) by it. The average speed formula `(2*s1*s2)/(s1+s2)` applies when equal distances are covered at speeds `s1` and `s2`, but here the speeds are `D` and `U`. The average speed would be `(2 * 12 * 8) / (12 + 8) = (192 / 20) = 9.6` km/hr. Total time = Total Distance / Average Speed = 96 / 9.6 = 10 hours. While this works, it's often faster and less error-prone to calculate individual times and sum them, especially if asked for only one leg of the journey. The direct summation `Time = Dist/(B+S) + Dist/(B-S)` is the most direct application.
