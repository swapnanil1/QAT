# Objects Moving in Same Direction Example

**Problem:**

A thief spots a policeman 150 meters away and starts running at a speed of 8 km/hr. Simultaneously, the policeman starts chasing the thief at a speed of 10 km/hr. How far will the thief have run before being caught by the policeman?

(A) 600 m
(B) 750 m
(C) 800 m
(D) 1000 m

## Solution:

This problem involves two objects (thief and policeman) moving in the same direction, with the faster one (policeman) chasing the slower one (thief). We need to use the relative speed concept for objects moving in the same direction, as discussed in [[11 - Time, Speed and Distance/03 - Relative Speed (Same Opposite Direction).md]].

**Step 1: Identify the Given Information**
Initial distance between thief and policeman = 150 meters.
Speed of the thief (S_thief) = 8 km/hr.
Speed of the policeman (S_police) = 10 km/hr.

**Step 2: Calculate the Relative Speed**
Since they are moving in the same direction, the relative speed is the difference between their speeds. The policeman is closing the gap at this relative speed.
Relative Speed (`S_rel`) = `S_police - S_thief`
`S_rel` = 10 km/hr - 8 km/hr = 2 km/hr.

**Step 3: Ensure Consistent Units**
The initial distance is in meters, but the speeds are in km/hr. It's best to convert the relative speed to m/s to match the distance unit and find time in seconds, or convert distance to km. Let's convert speed to m/s.
`S_rel` = 2 km/hr
Convert using `1 km/hr = 5/18 m/s`:
`S_rel` = `2 * (5/18)` m/s = `10/18` m/s = `5/9` m/s.

The initial distance to be covered at this relative speed is 150 m.

**Step 4: Calculate the Time to Catch**
Time = Distance / Relative Speed
Time (`T`) = 150 m / (5/9 m/s)
`T` = 150 * (9/5) seconds
`T` = (150/5) * 9 seconds
`T` = 30 * 9 = 270 seconds.

So, the policeman will catch the thief in 270 seconds.

**Step 5: Calculate the Distance Run by the Thief**
The question asks how far the thief ran before being caught. The thief ran at his own speed for the calculated time duration.
Speed of thief (S_thief) = 8 km/hr. Convert this to m/s:
`S_thief` = `8 * (5/18)` m/s = `40/18` m/s = `20/9` m/s.

Distance run by thief (`D_thief`) = `S_thief * Time`
`D_thief` = (20/9 m/s) * 270 seconds
`D_thief` = 20 * (270/9) meters
`D_thief` = 20 * 30 meters
`D_thief` = 600 meters.

The thief will have run 600 meters before being caught. This matches option (A).

**Alternative Calculation (Keeping Speed in km/hr):**
Convert distance to km: 150 m = 0.15 km.
Relative Speed = 2 km/hr.
Time to catch = Distance / Relative Speed = 0.15 km / 2 km/hr = 0.075 hours.
Distance run by thief = Speed of thief * Time = 8 km/hr * 0.075 hours
Distance run by thief = 8 * (75/1000) = 8 * (3/40) = 2 * (3/10) = 6/10 = 0.6 km.
0.6 km = 600 meters.

## Shortcut/Tip

1.  **Relative Speed:** Calculate the relative speed for the same direction: `S_rel = S_faster - S_slower = 10 - 8 = 2` km/hr.
2.  **Time to Catch:** Calculate the time taken to cover the initial distance at the relative speed: `Time = Initial Distance / S_rel`. Ensure units are consistent (e.g., `0.15 km / 2 km/hr = 0.075` hours).
3.  **Distance Covered:** The distance covered by *either* person is their *own speed* multiplied by the *time to catch*.
    *   Distance (Thief) = `S_thief * Time = 8 km/hr * 0.075 hr = 0.6` km = 600 m.
    *   Distance (Police) = `S_police * Time = 10 km/hr * 0.075 hr = 0.75` km = 750 m. (Note: 750 m - 600 m = 150 m, the initial gap).
4.  **Ratio Method:** The distances covered by the two objects in the same time are in the ratio of their speeds. Ratio of Speeds (Thief : Police) = 8 : 10 = 4 : 5. The policeman covers 1 part more in the ratio (5-4=1), which corresponds to the initial distance gap (150 m). So, 1 part = 150 m. The thief covers 4 parts, so Distance (Thief) = `4 * 150 = 600` m. This is often the fastest method.
