# Probability of 'A or B' (Not Mutually Exclusive) Example

**Problem:**
One card is drawn at random from a standard deck of 52 playing cards. What is the probability that the card drawn is either a King or a Heart?

(A) 4/13
(B) 1/13
(C) 17/52
(D) 16/52

## Solution:

This problem asks for the probability of drawing a card that belongs to one category (King) *or* another category (Heart). Since it's possible for a card to belong to both categories (the King of Hearts), these events are *not* mutually exclusive. We must use the general Addition Rule: `P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)`, as detailed in [[16 - Probability/02 - Probability Rules (Addition, Multiplication).md|the probability rules concept note]].

**Step 1: Identify the Sample Space (S)**
The experiment is drawing one card from a standard 52-card deck.
Total number of possible outcomes `n(S) = 52`.

**Step 2: Define the Events**
Event A: Drawing a King.
Event B: Drawing a Heart.

**Step 3: Calculate Individual Probabilities P(A) and P(B)**
`P(A)` (Probability of drawing a King): There are 4 Kings (King of Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades) in a deck.
`n(A) = 4`.
`P(A) = n(A) / n(S) = 4 / 52`.

`P(B)` (Probability of drawing a Heart): There are 13 Hearts in a deck (Ace through King).
`n(B) = 13`.
`P(B) = n(B) / n(S) = 13 / 52`.

**Step 4: Check for Mutual Exclusivity and Calculate P(A and B)**
Can events A and B happen at the same time? Yes, the King of Hearts is both a King and a Heart. Therefore, the events are *not* mutually exclusive.
Event (A and B): Drawing a card that is both a King AND a Heart. There is only one such card: the King of Hearts.
`n(A and B) = 1`.
`P(A and B) = n(A and B) / n(S) = 1 / 52`.

**Step 5: Apply the General Addition Rule**
The formula is `P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)`.
`P(King or Heart) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)`
`P(King or Heart) = (4 / 52) + (13 / 52) - (1 / 52)`
`P(King or Heart) = (4 + 13 - 1) / 52`
`P(King or Heart) = 16 / 52`

**Step 6: Simplify and State the Final Probability**
Simplify the fraction: Both 16 and 52 are divisible by 4.
`16 / 52 = 4 / 13`.
The probability of drawing a King or a Heart is 16/52 or 4/13. Option (A) represents the simplified fraction, while Option (D) is the unsimplified correct calculation. *In MCQ context, always check if the simplified form is listed. If both simplified and unsimplified correct answers are listed, it's ambiguous, but usually the simplified form is preferred. Here, 4/13 is present.*

Let's assume Option (A) is the intended answer based on simplification.

**Final Answer:** The probability is 4/13.

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **'OR' Keyword + Overlap = General Addition Rule:** Recognize "OR" means Addition Rule. Check if the events can overlap (non-mutually exclusive). If yes, use the full formula `P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)` to avoid double-counting the overlap.
2.  **Identify the Overlap:** Clearly identify the element(s) belonging to both events (here, the King of Hearts) to find `P(A and B)`.
3.  **Direct Counting:** Alternatively, count the favorable outcomes directly: There are 4 Kings and 13 Hearts. The King of Hearts is counted in both groups. So, total unique favorable cards = (Number of Kings) + (Number of Hearts) - (Number of Kings of Hearts) = 4 + 13 - 1 = 16 cards. Then `P(E) = 16 / 52 = 4 / 13`.
4.  **Standard Deck Knowledge:** Familiarity with a standard 52-card deck (4 suits: Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs, Spades; 13 ranks: A, 2-10, J, Q, K per suit; 2 colors: Red/Black) is essential for card problems.
5.  **Common Mistake:** Forgetting to subtract the overlap `P(A and B)` is a frequent error. Simply adding `P(A) + P(B)` would give `4/52 + 13/52 = 17/52` (Option C), which is incorrect because the King of Hearts is counted twice.
