# Probability of 'A or B' (Mutually Exclusive) Example

**Problem:**
A single fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a 2 or a 5?

(A) 1/6
(B) 1/3
(C) 1/2
(D) 1/36

## Solution:

This problem asks for the probability of one event *or* another event occurring. Since a single die roll cannot result in both a 2 and a 5 simultaneously, these events are mutually exclusive. We use the Addition Rule for mutually exclusive events as discussed in [[16 - Probability/02 - Probability Rules (Addition, Multiplication).md|the probability rules concept note]].

**Step 1: Identify the Sample Space (S)**
For a single die roll:
`S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}`
Total number of outcomes `n(S) = 6`.

**Step 2: Define the Events**
Event A: Rolling a 2. `A = {2}`.
Event B: Rolling a 5. `B = {5}`.

**Step 3: Calculate Individual Probabilities**
Using the basic probability formula `P(E) = n(E) / n(S)` from [[16 - Probability/01 - Basic Terminology (Event, Sample Space, P(E)).md|basic probability concepts]]:
`P(A) = n(A) / n(S) = 1 / 6`
`P(B) = n(B) / n(S) = 1 / 6`

**Step 4: Check for Mutual Exclusivity**
Can events A and B happen at the same time? No, a single roll cannot be both a 2 and a 5. Therefore, the events are mutually exclusive.

**Step 5: Apply the Addition Rule for Mutually Exclusive Events**
The formula is `P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)`.
`P(Rolling a 2 or 5) = P(A) + P(B)`
`P(Rolling a 2 or 5) = 1/6 + 1/6`
`P(Rolling a 2 or 5) = 2/6`

**Step 6: Simplify and State the Final Probability**
Simplify the fraction:
`P(Rolling a 2 or 5) = 1/3`
The probability of rolling a 2 or a 5 is 1/3. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **'OR' Keyword + Mutually Exclusive = Addition:** Recognize that "OR" implies the Addition Rule. If the events cannot happen together (mutually exclusive), simply add their individual probabilities: `P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)`.
2.  **Combine Favorable Outcomes:** Alternatively, directly count the total favorable outcomes for the combined event 'A or B'. Here, the favorable outcomes are {2, 5}. So `n(A or B) = 2`. Then `P(A or B) = n(A or B) / n(S) = 2 / 6 = 1/3`. This is often faster for simple cases.
3.  **Don't Subtract P(A and B):** Since the events are mutually exclusive, `P(A and B) = 0`. Using the general addition rule `P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)` would give `1/6 + 1/6 - 0 = 1/3`, but it's an unnecessary step here.
4.  **Distinguish 'OR' from 'AND':** Don't confuse the Addition Rule (for 'OR') with the Multiplication Rule (for 'AND'). The probability of rolling a 2 *and* a 5 on a single roll is 0. Multiplying probabilities (1/6 * 1/6 = 1/36 - Option D) would be incorrect for an 'OR' situation and typically applies to sequential or independent 'AND' events.
