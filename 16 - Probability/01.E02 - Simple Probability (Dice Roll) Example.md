# Simple Probability (Dice Roll) Example

**Problem:**
A single fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the probability of rolling a prime number?

(A) 1/6
(B) 1/3
(C) 1/2
(D) 2/3

## Solution:

This problem asks for the probability of a specific type of outcome (a prime number) when rolling a standard die. We will use the fundamental probability formula `P(E) = n(E) / n(S)` as described in [[16 - Probability/01 - Basic Terminology (Event, Sample Space, P(E)).md|the basic probability terminology note]].

**Step 1: Determine the Sample Space (S)**
When a single fair six-sided die is rolled, the possible outcomes are the numbers on its faces:
`S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}`

**Step 2: Find the Total Number of Outcomes (n(S))**
The total number of possible outcomes in the sample space is 6.
`n(S) = 6`.

**Step 3: Identify the Event (E)**
The event of interest is rolling a prime number. Recall that a prime number is a whole number greater than 1 that has only two divisors: 1 and itself. We need to identify the prime numbers within the sample space `S`.
The prime numbers in `S` are 2, 3, and 5.
(Note: 1 is not a prime number. 4 is divisible by 2. 6 is divisible by 2 and 3).
So, the event `E` is:
`E = {2, 3, 5}`

**Step 4: Find the Number of Favorable Outcomes (n(E))**
The number of outcomes favorable to event E (rolling a prime number) is 3.
`n(E) = 3`.

**Step 5: Calculate the Probability P(E)**
Using the formula `P(E) = n(E) / n(S)`:
`P(E) = 3 / 6`
`P(E) = 1/2`

**Step 6: State the Final Probability**
The probability of rolling a prime number when rolling a fair six-sided die is 1/2. This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Know Definitions:** Ensure you know the definitions of terms used in probability questions (e.g., prime numbers, even numbers, multiples). Forgetting that 1 is not prime is a common error.
2.  **List Sample Space:** For simple experiments like a single die roll, listing `S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}` is quick and helps visualize the outcomes.
3.  **Identify Event Outcomes Carefully:** Scan the sample space and pick out only those outcomes that match the event description (`E = {2, 3, 5}`).
4.  **Simplify Fraction:** Always simplify the final probability fraction (`3/6 = 1/2`).
5.  **Fair Die:** The term "fair" implies each outcome (1 through 6) is equally likely, justifying the use of the `n(E)/n(S)` formula.
6.  **Distractor Analysis:** Option (A) 1/6 is the probability of rolling *any specific* number. Option (B) 1/3 might result from incorrectly identifying only two prime numbers (e.g., missing 2 or 5) or perhaps counting 4 outcomes (e.g. wrongly including 1). Option (D) 2/3 corresponds to `4/6`, perhaps counting non-prime numbers (1, 4, 6) and calculating `1 - P(Non-prime)`, or incorrectly identifying four primes.
