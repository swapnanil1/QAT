# Drawing Balls from Bag Example

**Problem:**
A bag contains 5 Red, 4 Blue, and 3 Green balls. If 3 balls are drawn at random from the bag without replacement, what is the probability that exactly 1 is Red and 2 are Blue?

(A) 3/22
(B) 1/22
(C) 3/11
(D) 1/7

## Solution:

This problem involves calculating the probability of selecting a specific combination of colored balls when drawing multiple balls from a bag. Since the order of drawing the balls doesn't matter for the final group selected, we use combinations ([[15 - Permutations and Combinations/03 - Combination (Selection) Formula (nCr).md|nCr formula]]) to count the total and favorable outcomes, as outlined in [[16 - Probability/03 - Problems on Cards, Balls, etc.md|the concept note on common probability scenarios]].

**Step 1: Identify the Setup and Goal**
Total balls in the bag (N) = 5 Red + 4 Blue + 3 Green = 12 balls.
Number of balls drawn (r) = 3.
Event (E): Drawing exactly 1 Red ball AND 2 Blue balls.

**Step 2: Determine the Total Number of Outcomes (n(S))**
We are drawing 3 balls from a total of 12. The total number of ways to choose 3 balls from 12 is `12C3`.
`n(S) = 12C3 = 12! / (3! * (12 - 3)!) = 12! / (3! * 9!)`
`n(S) = (12 * 11 * 10 * 9!) / ((3 * 2 * 1) * 9!)`
`n(S) = (12 * 11 * 10) / 6`
`n(S) = 2 * 11 * 10 = 220`.

**Step 3: Determine the Number of Favorable Outcomes (n(E))**
The event requires selecting 1 Red ball from the 5 available Red balls AND selecting 2 Blue balls from the 4 available Blue balls.
Ways to choose 1 Red ball from 5 = `5C1`.
Ways to choose 2 Blue balls from 4 = `4C2`.
Since both conditions must be met ('AND'), we use the multiplication principle:
`n(E) = (Ways to choose 1 Red) * (Ways to choose 2 Blue)`
`n(E) = 5C1 * 4C2`

Calculate the combinations:
`5C1 = 5! / (1! * 4!) = 5`.
`4C2 = 4! / (2! * 2!) = (4 * 3 * 2!) / ((2 * 1) * 2!) = (4 * 3) / 2 = 6`.

Substitute back into the expression for `n(E)`:
`n(E) = 5 * 6 = 30`.

**Step 4: Calculate the Probability P(E)**
Using the formula `P(E) = n(E) / n(S)` from [[16 - Probability/01 - Basic Terminology (Event, Sample Space, P(E)).md|basic probability concepts]]:
`P(E) = 30 / 220`

**Step 5: Simplify and State the Final Probability**
Simplify the fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor (10):
`P(E) = 3 / 22`.
The probability of drawing exactly 1 Red and 2 Blue balls is 3/22. This matches option (A).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Combinations are Key:** For selection problems (cards, balls) where order doesn't matter, combinations (`nCr`) are the primary tool for counting `n(S)` and `n(E)`.
2.  **'AND' Means Multiply:** When an event requires satisfying multiple conditions simultaneously (e.g., 1 Red AND 2 Blue), calculate the number of ways for each condition using combinations and multiply the results to get `n(E)`.
3.  **Structure n(E):** `n(E)` = (Ways to choose required items of type 1) * (Ways to choose required items of type 2) * ...
4.  **Structure P(E):** `P(E) = [ (Group1_C_r1) * (Group2_C_r2) * ... ] / (Total_N_C_Total_r)`. Here: `[ 5C1 * 4C2 ] / 12C3`.
5.  **Simplify Calculations:** Break down combination calculations (`12C3 = (12*11*10)/(3*2*1)`) and simplify intermediate results.
6.  **Common Errors:** Using permutations incorrectly; adding combinations instead of multiplying for 'AND' conditions (`5C1 + 4C2 = 5+6=11`, leading to probability 11/220 = 1/20, not an option here but a common mistake type); errors in calculating combinations or final fraction simplification.
