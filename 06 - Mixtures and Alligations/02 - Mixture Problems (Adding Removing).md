# Mixture Problems (Adding / Removing)

#quant #mixtures_and_alligations #mixture_problems #ratio_concepts #percentage

This section deals with problems where the composition of a mixture changes due to the addition of one of its components (or a new component like water) or the removal of a portion of the mixture.

## 1. Adding a Component to a Mixture

When a quantity of one of the components (e.g., adding water to a milk-water solution, or adding more sugar to a syrup) is added to the mixture:

*   The quantity of the component being added increases.
*   The quantities of the other components remain the same.
*   The total quantity of the mixture increases.
*   The ratios and percentages of all components in the mixture change.

**Solving Strategy:**

1.  **Calculate Initial Quantities:** Determine the initial amounts of each component in the mixture using the given total quantity and ratio/percentage.
2.  **Calculate Changes:** Add the quantity of the substance being added to its initial amount. The other component quantities remain unchanged.
3.  **Calculate Final Quantities:** Find the new total quantity of the mixture and the new quantities of each component.
4.  **Determine Final Ratio/Percentage:** Calculate the new ratio or percentage concentration as required by the problem.

**Alternative Strategy (Focus on Invariant Quantity):**
Often, you are given the initial state and the final ratio/percentage, and asked how much substance was added.
1.  Identify the component whose quantity *does not* change during the process (the one *not* being added).
2.  Calculate the initial quantity of this invariant component.
3.  Use the *final* ratio/percentage to express the final quantity of the *other* component(s) relative to the invariant quantity.
4.  Calculate the final total quantity or the final quantity of the component that was added.
5.  The difference between the final and initial quantity of the added component gives the answer.

**Example:** 40 litres of a milk-water mixture contains 10% water. How much water must be added to make the water content 20% in the new mixture?
1.  Initial: Total=40L. Water = 10% of 40 = 4L. Milk = 90% of 40 = 36L.
2.  Let `x` litres of water be added.
3.  Final: Milk = 36L (invariant). Water = (4+x)L. Total = (40+x)L.
4.  Final water % = (Final Water / Final Total) * 100 = 20%
    `(4+x) / (40+x) = 20 / 100 = 1/5`
    `5 * (4+x) = 1 * (40+x)`
    `20 + 5x = 40 + x`
    `4x = 20`
    `x = 5` litres.
    So, 5 litres of water must be added.

## 2. Removing a Portion of the Mixture

When a portion of a homogeneous mixture is removed:

*   **Key Principle:** The ratio and percentage concentration of the components in the remaining mixture are *exactly the same* as in the original mixture.
*   The total quantity of the mixture decreases.
*   The absolute quantities of each component decrease proportionally.

**Example:** A 60-litre mixture of milk and water is in the ratio 2:1 (Milk=40L, Water=20L). If 15 litres of this mixture are removed, what is the ratio of milk to water in the remaining mixture?
1.  Original Ratio = 2:1.
2.  Amount removed = 15L.
3.  Remaining Total Quantity = 60 - 15 = 45L.
4.  The ratio in the remaining 45L is still **2:1**.
5.  (Optional Calculation): ```
```text
Amount removed 15L. 
Milk removed = (2/3)*15=10L. 
Water removed = (1/3)*15=5L.
Remaining Milk = 40-10=30L. 
Remaining Water = 20-5=15L.
Remaining ratio = 30:15 = 2:1. 
Yes, the ratio remains the same.
```

**Important Note:** This concept is often the first step in *replacement* problems, where the removed portion is replaced by something else (usually one of the components, like water). Replacement problems change the final ratio and are covered in [[06 - Mixtures and Alligations/03 - Successive Dilution Replacement.md]].
