## Problem:

A merchant has two varieties of sugar. Variety A costs ₹ 40 per kg and Variety B costs ₹ 55 per kg. He mixes them in a certain ratio and sells the mixture at ₹ 54 per kg, making a profit of 12.5%. In what ratio did he mix the two varieties (A : B)?

(A) 2 : 1
(B) 7 : 8
(C) 8 : 7
(D) 1 : 2

## Solution:

**1. Calculate the Cost Price (CP) of the mixture:**
   The mixture is sold at a Selling Price (SP) of ₹ 54 per kg with a profit of 12.5%.
   Profit percentage = 12.5% = 1/8.
   The formula relating SP, CP, and Profit % is `SP = CP * (1 + Profit%)`.
   Alternatively, `CP = SP / (1 + Profit%)`.

   `CP = 54 / (1 + 1/8)`
   `CP = 54 / (9/8)`
   `CP = 54 * (8/9)`
   `CP = (54 / 9) * 8`
   `CP = 6 * 8`
   `CP = 48`
   So, the mean Cost Price (M) of the mixture is ₹ 48 per kg.

**2. Identify the costs of the ingredients and the mean cost:**
   *   Cost of Cheaper Variety (A) = C = ₹ 40 per kg
   *   Cost of Dearer Variety (B) = D = ₹ 55 per kg
   *   Mean Cost Price of Mixture = M = ₹ 48 per kg

**3. Apply the Rule of Alligation:**

   ```text
    Variety A (Cheaper)        Variety B (Dearer)
         C = 40                   D = 55
           \                       /
            \                     /
              Mean Cost (M = 48)
            /                     \
           /                       \
      (D - M) = 55 - 48         (M - C) = 48 - 40
          = 7                       = 8
          |                         |
          v                         v
    Ratio Part for A           Ratio Part for B
```

**4. Determine the ratio:**  
The ratio in which Variety A (Cheaper) and Variety B (Dearer) are mixed is given by:  
Ratio (A : B) = (D - M) : (M - C)  
Ratio (A : B) = 7 : 8
Therefore, the merchant mixed Variety A and Variety B in the ratio 7 : 8.
## Shortcut/Tip

- **CP is Key:** Never apply alligation directly on Selling Price if profit or loss is involved. Always calculate the Cost Price of the mixture first. This is the most common trap in such problems.
    
- **Alligation Diagram:** The visual diagram helps organize the values (C, D, M) and correctly calculate the differences (D-M and M-C) which form the ratio. Remember the criss-cross subtraction.
    
- **Ratio Order:** The difference calculated under the Dearer price (D-M) corresponds to the quantity/ratio part of the Cheaper ingredient, and the difference calculated under the Cheaper price (M-C) corresponds to the quantity/ratio part of the Dearer ingredient. The final ratio required (A:B) is (D-M) : (M-C).
    
- **Fraction Profit:** Convert percentages like 12.5% to fractions (1/8) for easier calculation. 1 + 1/8 = 9/8.
    
- **Verification:** Ensure the mean price (48) lies between the two initial prices (40 and 55). If not, there's an error.
