# Calculation based on Table (Average, Ratio) Example

**Problem:**

The table below shows the production figures (in units) for three types of vehicles by three different companies in the year 2023.

| Company | Cars Produced | Bikes Produced | Trucks Produced |
| :------ | :------------ | :------------- | :-------------- |
| Alpha   | 5000          | 12000          | 3000            |
| Beta    | 6000          | 10000          | 4500            |
| Gamma   | 4000          | 11000          | 2500            |

What is the ratio of the average number of Cars produced by the three companies to the number of Trucks produced by Company Beta?

(A) 1 : 1
(B) 9 : 10
(C) 10 : 9
(D) 10 : 3

## Solution:

This problem requires calculating an average from table data and then finding a ratio involving this average and another specific data point, based on the methods described in [[17 - Data Interpretation (DI)/01 - Tables Interpretation.md|Tables Interpretation]].

**Step 1:** Calculate the total number of Cars produced by all three companies.
   - Locate the "Cars Produced" column.
   - Sum the values for Alpha, Beta, and Gamma:
   Total Cars = 5000 + 6000 + 4000 = 15000 units.

**Step 2:** Calculate the average number of Cars produced.
   - Use the [[08 - Averages/01 - Basic Average Formula.md|basic average formula]]: `Average = Total Sum / Number of Items`.
   - Number of companies = 3.
   Average Cars Produced = 15000 / 3 = 5000 units.

**Step 3:** Identify the number of Trucks produced by Company Beta.
   - Locate the row for "Beta" and the column for "Trucks Produced".
   - The value at the intersection is 4500 units.

**Step 4:** Calculate the required ratio.
   - The question asks for the ratio of (Average Cars Produced) to (Trucks Produced by Beta).
   - Ratio = 5000 : 4500

**Step 5:** Simplify the ratio.
   - Divide both sides of the ratio by their greatest common divisor. Both are divisible by 500.
   - Ratio = (5000 / 500) : (4500 / 500) = 10 : 9
   - This uses the concept of simplifying ratios covered in [[05 - Ratio and Proportion/01 - Ratio Concepts (Duplicate, Triplicate, etc).md|Ratio Concepts]].

This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Targeted Calculation:** In MCQs, focus only on the required calculations. Calculate the average cars (5000) and find Beta's trucks (4500). Don't calculate other averages or totals unless necessary.
*   **Efficient Ratio Simplification:** When forming the ratio 5000 : 4500, immediately cancel common trailing zeros: 50 : 45. Then, identify the common factor (5): (50/5) : (45/5) = 10 : 9. This is faster than finding the G.C.D. explicitly.
*   **Pitfall - Average vs. Total:** A very common error is using the *total* cars (15000) instead of the *average* (5000) for the ratio. If you calculate 15000 : 4500, it simplifies to 150 : 45 -> 30 : 9 -> 10 : 3. Notice this leads directly to distractor (D). Always double-check if the question asks for 'average' or 'total'.
*   **Pitfall - Ratio Order:** The question asks for (Average Cars) : (Beta Trucks). Ensure your final ratio is in this order (10 : 9). Calculating Beta Trucks : Average Cars would give 4500 : 5000, simplifying to 9 : 10, which matches distractor (B). Reading the ratio order correctly is crucial.
*   **Distractor Analysis:** Option (A) 1:1 might tempt someone who incorrectly calculates the average or misreads a value, perhaps confusing Beta's Cars (6000) or Alpha's Cars (5000) with the average or Beta's trucks. Options (B) and (D) are designed to catch the specific pitfalls mentioned above (ratio inversion and using total instead of average). Recognizing these potential traps helps confirm the correct approach.
