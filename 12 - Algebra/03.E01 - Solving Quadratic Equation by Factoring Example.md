# Solving Quadratic Equation by Factoring Example

**Problem:**
Find the roots of the quadratic equation `x^2 - 7x + 12 = 0`.

(A) x = -3, x = -4
(B) x = 3, x = 4
(C) x = 2, x = 6
(D) x = -2, x = -6

## Solution:

This problem asks for the roots of a quadratic equation. Since it might be factorable, we can attempt the factorization method first, as described in [[12 - Algebra/03 - Quadratic Equations.md]]. The equation is already in the standard form `ax^2 + bx + c = 0`, with `a=1`, `b=-7`, and `c=12`.

**Step 1: Find two numbers**
We need to find two numbers that:
*   Multiply to `a*c` = 1 * 12 = 12
*   Add up to `b` = -7

Let's list pairs of factors of 12: (1, 12), (2, 6), (3, 4), (-1, -12), (-2, -6), (-3, -4).
Check their sums:
1 + 12 = 13
2 + 6 = 8
3 + 4 = 7
-1 + (-12) = -13
-2 + (-6) = -8
-3 + (-4) = -7

The pair -3 and -4 satisfies both conditions (multiply to 12, add to -7).

**Step 2: Split the middle term**
Rewrite the middle term `-7x` using the numbers found: `-3x - 4x`.
The equation becomes: `x^2 - 3x - 4x + 12 = 0`

**Step 3: Factor by grouping**
Group the first two terms and the last two terms:
`(x^2 - 3x) + (-4x + 12) = 0`
Factor out the greatest common factor (GCF) from each group:
`x(x - 3) - 4(x - 3) = 0`
*(Note the sign change when factoring out -4 from +12).*

**Step 4: Factor out the common binomial**
The term `(x - 3)` is common to both parts. Factor it out:
`(x - 3)(x - 4) = 0`

**Step 5: Apply the Zero-Product Property**
If the product of two factors is zero, at least one of them must be zero.
So, either `x - 3 = 0` or `x - 4 = 0`.

**Step 6: Solve for x**
Solve the two linear equations:
*   `x - 3 = 0`  =>  `x = 3`
*   `x - 4 = 0`  =>  `x = 4`

The roots of the equation are `x = 3` and `x = 4`. This matches option (B).

*(Self-check: Substitute x=3: `3^2 - 7*3 + 12 = 9 - 21 + 12 = 0`. Substitute x=4: `4^2 - 7*4 + 12 = 16 - 28 + 12 = 0`. Both roots work.)*

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Identify `a, b, c`:** Quickly determine the coefficients from the standard form `ax^2 + bx + c = 0`.
2.  **Find the Pair:** Focus on finding the two numbers that multiply to `ac` and add to `b`. This is the core of factoring trinomials.
3.  **Factor Directly (if a=1):** When `a=1`, if you find the two numbers (say `p` and `q`), the factored form is directly `(x+p)(x+q)`. In our case, the numbers were -3 and -4, so the factors are `(x-3)(x-4)`.
4.  **Zero-Product:** Set each factor to zero and solve the simple linear equations. `(x-3)=0` gives `x=3`, `(x-4)=0` gives `x=4`.
5.  **Sign Errors:** Be extremely careful with signs when finding the number pair and during factoring by grouping. Option (A) results from a sign error.
