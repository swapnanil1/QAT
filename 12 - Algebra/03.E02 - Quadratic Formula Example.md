# Quadratic Formula Example

**Problem:**
Find the roots of the quadratic equation `2x^2 + 5x - 4 = 0` using the quadratic formula.

(A) `x = (-5 ± sqrt(57)) / 4`
(B) `x = (5 ± sqrt(57)) / 4`
(C) `x = (-5 ± sqrt(-7)) / 4`
(D) `x = (-5 ± sqrt(57)) / 2`

## Solution:

This problem asks for the roots of a quadratic equation. The expression `2x^2 + 5x - 4` is not easily factorable by finding integer pairs (since `a*c = 2*(-4) = -8`, and no integer factors of -8 add up to 5). Therefore, we will use the quadratic formula as described in [[12 - Algebra/03 - Quadratic Equations.md]].

The equation is in the standard form `ax^2 + bx + c = 0`.

**Step 1: Identify Coefficients**
*   `a = 2`
*   `b = 5`
*   `c = -4`

**Step 2: State the Quadratic Formula**
The formula for the roots `x` is:
`x = [-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)] / (2a)`

**Step 3: Calculate the Discriminant (Optional but helpful)**
The discriminant `Δ = b^2 - 4ac` determines the nature of the roots and simplifies the formula calculation.
`Δ` = 5^2 - 4 * (2) * (-4)
`Δ` = 25 - (-32)
`Δ` = 25 + 32
`Δ` = 57
Since `Δ > 0`, we expect two distinct real roots.

**Step 4: Substitute Coefficients into the Formula**
Substitute the values of `a`, `b`, `c`, and the discriminant (`Δ = 57`) into the formula:
`x = [-(5) ± sqrt(57)] / (2 * 2)`

**Step 5: Simplify the Expression**
`x = [-5 ± sqrt(57)] / 4`

**Step 6: State the Roots**
The two roots are:
*   `x1 = (-5 + sqrt(57)) / 4`
*   `x2 = (-5 - sqrt(57)) / 4`

These are the exact roots of the equation. The combined expression `x = (-5 ± sqrt(57)) / 4` matches option (A).

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **When to Use:** The quadratic formula is essential when factoring is not obvious or possible with integers, or when the roots are irrational or complex.
2.  **Calculate Discriminant First:** Calculating `Δ = b^2 - 4ac` separately helps avoid errors within the main formula calculation and immediately tells you the nature of the roots.
3.  **Sign Errors:** Be extremely careful with signs:
    *   The `-b` term: If `b` is positive, `-b` is negative. If `b` is negative, `-b` becomes positive. (Option B has this error).
    *   The `-4ac` term: Pay close attention to the sign of `c`. If `c` is negative, `-4ac` becomes positive. (Option C makes an error here).
4.  **Denominator:** Remember the denominator is `2a`, not just `2` or `a`. (Option D makes this error).
5.  **Simplifying Surds:** If the discriminant is a perfect square, the roots will be rational. If not (like `sqrt(57)` here), leave it as a surd unless asked for an approximation.
