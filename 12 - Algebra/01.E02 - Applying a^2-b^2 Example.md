# Applying a^2-b^2 Example

**Problem:**
Factorize the expression `9x^2 - 16y^2`.

(A) `(3x - 4y)(3x - 4y)`
(B) `(9x - 16y)(x + y)`
(C) `(3x + 4y)(3x - 4y)`
(D) `(9x + 16y)(x - y)`

## Solution:

This problem requires factorising an expression that fits the "difference of squares" pattern. We will use the algebraic identity `a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)`, as discussed in [[12 - Algebra/01 - Basic Algebraic Identities.md]].

**Step 1: Recognize the Difference of Squares Pattern**
The expression `9x^2 - 16y^2` involves the subtraction of two terms, and both terms can be expressed as perfect squares.
*   `9x^2` is `(3x)^2`
*   `16y^2` is `(4y)^2`

**Step 2: Identify 'a' and 'b'**
Comparing `9x^2 - 16y^2` to the identity `a^2 - b^2`:
*   `a^2` corresponds to `9x^2`, so `a = sqrt(9x^2) = 3x`.
*   `b^2` corresponds to `16y^2`, so `b = sqrt(16y^2) = 4y`.

**Step 3: Apply the Identity Formula**
Substitute `a = 3x` and `b = 4y` into the factorization `(a+b)(a-b)`:
`9x^2 - 16y^2 = (3x + 4y)(3x - 4y)`

**Step 4: Verify the Result**
The factorization `(3x + 4y)(3x - 4y)` matches option (C).

*(Self-check by expanding: `(3x + 4y)(3x - 4y) = 3x*(3x - 4y) + 4y*(3x - 4y) = 9x^2 - 12xy + 12xy - 16y^2 = 9x^2 - 16y^2`. Matches the original expression.)*

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Spot the Pattern:** Look for exactly two terms, separated by a minus sign, where both terms are perfect squares (including their coefficients and variables with even powers).
2.  **Find Roots:** Take the square root of the first term (`sqrt(9x^2) = 3x`) and the square root of the second term (`sqrt(16y^2) = 4y`).
3.  **Apply Formula:** Write the factors directly as `(Root1 + Root2)(Root1 - Root2)`. Here, `(3x + 4y)(3x - 4y)`.
4.  **Numerical Application:** This identity is also very useful for quick calculations, e.g., 105^2 - 95^2 = (105 + 95)(105 - 95) = (200)(10) = 2000.
5.  **Common Errors:**
    *   Mistaking it for `(a-b)^2`, which is `a^2 - 2ab + b^2` (leading to option A).
    *   Not taking the square root of the coefficients (resulting in incorrect factors like in options B and D).
    *   Applying it when there's a plus sign (`a^2 + b^2` generally does not factor easily with real numbers).
