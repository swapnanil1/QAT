# Solving Linear Equation in One Variable Example

**Problem:**
Find the value of `x` that satisfies the equation: `5(x - 3) - 2(x + 1) = 7`

(A) 6
(B) 8
(C) 7
(D) 9

## Solution:

This problem involves solving a linear equation with one variable, `x`. The goal is to isolate `x` on one side of the equation using algebraic manipulations, as outlined in [[12 - Algebra/02 - Linear Equations (One Two Variables).md]].

**Step 1: Distribute the constants**
Multiply the constants outside the parentheses into the terms inside:
`5 * x + 5 * (-3) - 2 * x - 2 * (+1) = 7`
`5x - 15 - 2x - 2 = 7`

**Step 2: Combine like terms**
Group the `x` terms together and the constant terms together on the left side:
`(5x - 2x) + (-15 - 2) = 7`
`3x - 17 = 7`

**Step 3: Isolate the x term**
Add 17 to both sides of the equation to move the constant term to the right side:
`3x - 17 + 17 = 7 + 17`
`3x = 24`

**Step 4: Solve for x**
Divide both sides by the coefficient of `x` (which is 3):
`3x / 3 = 24 / 3`
`x = 8`

The value of `x` that satisfies the equation is 8. This matches option (B).

*(Self-check: Substitute x=8 back into the original equation: `5(8 - 3) - 2(8 + 1) = 5(5) - 2(9) = 25 - 18 = 7`. This matches the right side, so the solution is correct.)*

## Shortcut/Tip
1.  **Distribution Accuracy:** Be very careful with signs when distributing, especially when a negative number is outside the parenthesis (like the `-2` in `-2(x + 1)` becoming `-2x - 2`). This is a common source of errors.
2.  **Combine Systematically:** Combine all `x` terms first, then all constant terms on one side before moving terms across the equals sign.
3.  **Inverse Operations:** Remember to use the *inverse* operation to move terms: addition undoes subtraction, multiplication undoes division, and vice-versa.
4.  **Check Your Answer:** If time permits, quickly substitute your answer back into the original equation to verify it's correct. This is especially useful in MCQ tests to catch simple calculation mistakes.
