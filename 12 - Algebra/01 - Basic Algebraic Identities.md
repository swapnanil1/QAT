# Basic Algebraic Identities

Tags: #quant #algebra #basic_algebraic_identities #formula #concept #simplification

---

**Introduction:**

Algebraic identities are equations that hold true for all possible values of their variables. They represent fundamental relationships between algebraic expressions. In quantitative aptitude, recognising and applying these identities is crucial for simplifying complex expressions quickly and efficiently, often bypassing lengthy calculations. They form the bedrock for solving many problems in algebra and other related topics.

**Key Identities:**

Here are some of the most commonly used algebraic identities:

1.  **`(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2`**
2.  **`(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2`**
3.  **`a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)`** (Difference of Squares)
4.  **`(a+b+c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2ab + 2bc + 2ca`**
5.  **`(a+b)^3 = a^3 + b^3 + 3ab(a+b)`** or **`a^3 + 3a^2b + 3ab^2 + b^3`**
6.  **`(a-b)^3 = a^3 - b^3 - 3ab(a-b)`** or **`a^3 - 3a^2b + 3ab^2 - b^3`**
7.  **`a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)`** (Sum of Cubes)
8.  **`a^3 - b^3 = (a-b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)`** (Difference of Cubes)
9.  **(Less Common, but useful):** `a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = (a+b+c)(a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - ab - bc - ca)`
    *   *Special Case:* If `a+b+c = 0`, then `a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc`

**Importance and Application:**

*   **Simplification:** Identities allow complex-looking expressions to be condensed or expanded into simpler forms. This is particularly useful in [[01 - Number System/08 - Simplification and Approximation.md]] problems.
*   **Factorization:** Identities like the difference of squares (`a^2 - b^2`) or sum/difference of cubes are key tools for factoring expressions.
*   **Solving Equations:** They are fundamental in manipulating and solving equations, including [[12 - Algebra/03 - Quadratic Equations.md]].
*   **Problem Solving:** Many quantitative problems are designed such that applying an identity provides a significant shortcut. For instance, calculating `103^2` is easier using `(100+3)^2` or finding `99 * 101` using `(100-1)(100+1)`.

**General Approach:**

1.  **Memorise:** Learn the standard identities thoroughly.
2.  **Recognise Patterns:** Practice identifying expressions that match the structure of these identities (e.g., seeing `x^2 - 9` as `x^2 - 3^2`).
3.  **Substitute and Apply:** Substitute the corresponding parts of the expression into the identity formula (e.g., `a=x`, `b=3` in the previous example).
4.  **Simplify:** Expand or factorise according to the identity to reach the desired form.
