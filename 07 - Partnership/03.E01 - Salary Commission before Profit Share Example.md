# Salary Commission before Profit Share Example

Parent Concept: [[07 - Partnership/03 - Working and Sleeping Partners.md]]

#quant #partnership #compound_partnership #profit_sharing #working_partner #salary #commission #example

## Problem:

Arun and Bala enter into a partnership. Arun invests ₹ 40,000 and Bala invests ₹ 60,000. Arun is a working partner and is entitled to receive a commission of 10% of the total profit for managing the business. The remaining profit is distributed between Arun and Bala in the ratio of their investments. If the total profit at the end of the year is ₹ 50,000, what is Bala's share of the profit?

(A) ₹ 18,000
(B) ₹ 30,000
(C) ₹ 27,000
(D) ₹ 25,000

## Solution:

**1. Calculate the Working Partner's Commission:**
   Total Profit = ₹ 50,000.
   Arun's commission = 10% of Total Profit
   Arun's commission = 0.10 * 50000 = ₹ 5,000.

**2. Calculate the Remaining Profit for Distribution:**
   Remaining Profit = Total Profit - Arun's Commission
   Remaining Profit = 50000 - 5000 = ₹ 45,000.

**3. Determine the Profit Sharing Ratio based on Investments:**
   Investment Ratio (Arun : Bala) = `40000 : 60000`
   Simplify the ratio:
   Ratio = `40 : 60`
   Ratio = `4 : 6`
   Simplified Ratio = `2 : 3`

**4. Calculate the Sum of Ratio Parts:**
   Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5 parts.

**5. Distribute the Remaining Profit:**
   The remaining profit of ₹ 45,000 is distributed in the ratio 2 : 3.
   Value of 1 part = Remaining Profit / Total Parts
   Value of 1 part = 45000 / 5 = ₹ 9,000.

   *   Arun's share from remaining profit = 2 parts * 9000 = ₹ 18,000.
   *   Bala's share from remaining profit = 3 parts * 9000 = ₹ 27,000.

**6. Determine Bala's Total Share:**
   Bala is a sleeping partner (or at least not mentioned as receiving commission/salary). Her total share is only the share received from the distribution of the remaining profit.
   Bala's Total Share = ₹ 27,000.

(Arun's total earnings would be his commission plus his share: 5000 + 18000 = ₹ 23,000).

Therefore, Bala's share of the profit is ₹ 27,000.

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Subtract Remuneration First:** Always calculate and subtract any salary, commission, or fixed payment due to working partners *before* calculating the profit shares based on the investment ratio. A common error is to distribute the total profit according to the ratio first.
*   **Apply Ratio to Remaining Profit:** The investment ratio (2:3 in this case) applies *only* to the profit remaining *after* deducting the working partner's remuneration (₹ 45,000 here).
*   **Read Carefully:** Understand who is the working partner and what their remuneration is (salary, % commission, etc.). Note what the question asks for (specific partner's share, total earnings of a working partner, etc.). Bala's share is just her portion of the distributed profit, while Arun's total earning includes the commission.

## Why this is a good example for working and sleeping partners

1. **Working Partner Feature:** It clearly defines Arun as a working partner who receives remuneration (10% commission) for his active role, separate from his investment-based profit share.
    
2. **Remuneration Calculation:** It correctly demonstrates the crucial step of calculating and deducting the working partner's commission before distributing the remaining profit.
    
3. **Sleeping Partner Implication:** Although not explicitly named "sleeping", Bala functions as one in this context. Her share is determined only by her investment ratio applied to the profit that remains after Arun's commission is paid.
    
4. **Core Concept Demonstration:** The example directly applies the main principle explained in the concept note: separate the remuneration for work, then distribute the balance based on capital contributions.
