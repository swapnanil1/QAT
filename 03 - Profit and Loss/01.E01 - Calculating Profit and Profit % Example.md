# Example: Calculating Profit and Profit Percentage (with Overheads)

Tags: #quant #profit_and_loss #basic_terminology #profit #profit_percentage #cost_price #selling_price #example #overhead_costs

Parent Concept: [[03 - Profit and Loss/01 - Basic Terminology (CP, SP, Profit, Loss)]]

## Problem

A shopkeeper buys an old air conditioner for ₹8,000. He spends ₹500 on its transportation and ₹1,500 on its repair. If he sells the air conditioner for ₹12,000, calculate his profit and profit percentage.

## Analysis

This problem requires calculating the profit and profit percentage. The crucial first step is to determine the *total* Cost Price (CP) by including the purchase price and all overhead expenses (transportation and repairs). Profit and Profit% are then calculated based on this total CP.

## Solution

1.  **Calculate the Total Cost Price (Effective CP):**
    The total cost includes the purchase price plus all additional expenses incurred before selling.
    *   Purchase Price = ₹8,000
    *   Transportation Cost = ₹500
    *   Repair Cost = ₹1,500
    *   Total CP = Purchase Price + Transportation + Repairs
    *   Total CP = ₹8,000 + ₹500 + ₹1,500 = ₹10,000

2.  **Identify the Selling Price (SP):**
    *   SP = ₹12,000

3.  **Check for Profit or Loss:**
    *   Compare SP and Total CP: SP (₹12,000) > Total CP (₹10,000).
    *   Therefore, there is a profit.

4.  **Calculate the Profit Amount:**
    *   Profit = SP - Total CP
    *   Profit = ₹12,000 - ₹10,000 = ₹2,000

5.  **Calculate the Profit Percentage:**
    *   Profit % is calculated based on the Total CP.
    *   Profit % = (Profit / Total CP) * 100 %
    *   Profit % = (₹2,000 / ₹10,000) * 100 %
    *   Profit % = (2 / 10) * 100 %
    *   Profit % = (1 / 5) * 100 %
    *   Profit % = 20 %

## Shortcut/Tip

*   Always combine the basic cost with *all* overhead expenses to find the true Cost Price before calculating profit/loss or their percentages. Ignoring overheads is a common mistake.
*   Remember that Profit % = (Profit / CP) * 100. Simplify the fraction (Profit/CP) before multiplying by 100 (e.g., 2000/10000 simplifies to 1/5).

**Answer:** The shopkeeper made a profit of ₹2,000, which is a profit percentage of 20%.
