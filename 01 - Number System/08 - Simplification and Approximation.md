# 08 - Simplification and Approximation

**Tags:** #quant #number_system #simplification #approximation #vbomas #bodmas #roots #calculation_shortcuts

Simplification involves reducing complex arithmetic expressions to their simplest form using mathematical rules. Approximation involves finding a value close to the exact answer when precise calculation is difficult or unnecessary, often used with complex decimals, fractions, or roots. Both are vital skills for saving time in quantitative aptitude exams.

---

## Simplification: Order of Operations (VBODMAS/BODMAS)

To ensure consistent results when evaluating expressions with multiple operations, follow the VBODMAS rule:

*   **V** - Vinculum (or Bar): Solve expressions under a bar first. (Less common)
*   **B** - Brackets/Parentheses: Solve operations within brackets `()`, then curly braces `{}`, then square brackets `[]`, working from innermost to outermost.
*   **O** - Order/Of: Evaluate exponents (powers, square roots, cube roots) and operations denoted by 'Of' (which means multiplication).
*   **D** - Division (`/`, `÷`)
*   **M** - Multiplication (`*`, `×`)
    *   *(Division and Multiplication have equal precedence; solve from left to right)*
*   **A** - Addition (`+`)
*   **S** - Subtraction (`-`)
    *   *(Addition and Subtraction have equal precedence; solve from left to right)*

## Approximation Techniques

Used when exact values are hard to compute or when answer options are far apart.

1.  **Rounding:**
    *   Round numbers to the nearest convenient whole number, tens, hundreds, etc., or to a specific number of decimal places/significant figures.
    *   Be mindful of the degree of approximation – rounding too much can lead to inaccurate results.
2.  **Percentage Approximation:**
    *   Approximate percentages to easier fractions (e.g., 49% ≈ 50% = 1/2, 34% ≈ 33.33% = 1/3). See [[02 - Percentages/01 - Basic Concepts and Conversions]].
    *   Example: 24% of 810 ≈ 25% of 800 = (1/4) * 800 = 200.
3.  **Fraction Approximation:**
    *   Approximate complex fractions or decimals to nearby simpler fractions (e.g., 0.67 ≈ 2/3, 0.49 ≈ 1/2).

## Shortcuts for Square Roots and Cube Roots (Perfect Roots)

Finding exact roots of large perfect squares/cubes quickly.

**1. Square Root Estimation:**
*   **Units Digit:** The unit digit of the square determines the possible unit digit(s) of the root (e.g., square ends in 6 -> root ends in 4 or 6; see [[05 - Units Digit]]).
*   **Grouping:** Ignore the last two digits of the perfect square.
*   **First Part:** Find the largest integer whose square is less than or equal to the remaining number (left part). This forms the first part of the root.
*   **Deciding Last Digit:** Use the next consecutive square or a midpoint (number ending in 5) to decide between the possible unit digits.

**2. Cube Root Estimation:**
*   **Units Digit:** The unit digit of the perfect cube uniquely determines the unit digit of the cube root (e.g., cube ends in 7 -> root ends in 3; cube ends in 8 -> root ends in 2).
*   **Grouping:** Ignore the last three digits of the perfect cube.
*   **First Part:** Find the largest integer whose cube is less than or equal to the remaining number (left part). This forms the first part of the root.
*   Combine the first part and the determined unit digit.

*See Example:* [[08.E03 - Square Root Cube Root Shortcut Example]]
*   For non-perfect roots or verification, use prime factorization ([[02 - Factors and Multiples]]). Understanding [[12 - Algebra/04 - Indices and Surds]] is also helpful.

## General Tips for Speed and Accuracy

*   **Mental Math:** Practice basic addition, subtraction, multiplication tables, squares (up to 30), cubes (up to 15), and common fraction/percentage equivalents.
*   **Avoid Unnecessary Steps:** Look for cancellations or simplifications before performing complex multiplications or divisions.
*   **Convert to Fractions:** Operations are often easier with fractions than decimals.
*   **Use Divisibility Rules:** Quickly check potential factors ([[01 - Divisibility Rules]]).
*   **Check with Digital Roots:** Verify calculations quickly using [[07 - Digital Root (Casting Out Nines)]].
*   **Understand Indices:** Apply laws of exponents correctly ([[12 - Algebra/04 - Indices and Surds]]).
*   **Recognize Identities:** Sometimes algebraic identities simplify arithmetic ([[12 - Algebra/01 - Basic Algebraic Identities]]).
