# Unit Digit of Large Power (Cycle 4) Example

**Problem:**

What is the unit digit of 137^453?

(A) 1
(B) 3
(C) 7
(D) 9

## Solution:

To find the unit digit of 137^453, we only need to focus on the unit digit of the base, which is 7. We need to find the pattern (cyclicity) of the unit digits of powers of 7, as explained in the parent concept [[01 - Number System/05 - Units Digit.md]] (Details the concept of cyclicity for unit digits).

**Step 1: Determine the cyclicity of the unit digit 7.**
   Calculate the unit digits of the first few powers of 7:
   - Unit digit of 7^1 = 7
   - Unit digit of 7^2 = Unit digit of (7 * 7) = Unit digit of 49 = 9
   - Unit digit of 7^3 = Unit digit of (7^2 * 7) = Unit digit of (9 * 7) = Unit digit of 63 = 3
   - Unit digit of 7^4 = Unit digit of (7^3 * 7) = Unit digit of (3 * 7) = Unit digit of 21 = 1
   - Unit digit of 7^5 = Unit digit of (7^4 * 7) = Unit digit of (1 * 7) = 7

The pattern of the unit digits is (7, 9, 3, 1), and it repeats every 4 powers.
The cyclicity (cycle length) for the unit digit 7 is 4.

**Step 2: Find the remainder of the exponent when divided by the cycle length.**
The exponent is 453. The cycle length is 4. We need to find the remainder of 453 divided by 4.
453 mod 4 = Rem(453 / 4).
Since divisibility by 4 depends only on the last two digits, we find Rem(53 / 4).
53 = 13 * 4 + 1.
The remainder is 1.

**Step 3: Determine the unit digit based on the remainder.**
The remainder of the exponent (1) tells us which position in the cycle determines the final unit digit. A remainder of 1 corresponds to the 1st position in the cycle (7, 9, 3, 1).
The unit digit is the 1st unit digit in the cycle, which is 7.

Therefore, the unit digit of 137^453 is 7.

This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Focus on Base's Unit Digit:** The unit digit of 137^453 is determined solely by the unit digit of the base (7). The '13' part is irrelevant.
*   **Memorize Cycles (Optional but helpful):**
    *   Cycle length 1: 0, 1, 5, 6 (Unit digits remain the same)
    *   Cycle length 2: 4 (4, 6), 9 (9, 1)
    *   Cycle length 4: 2 (2, 4, 8, 6), 3 (3, 9, 7, 1), 7 (7, 9, 3, 1), 8 (8, 4, 2, 6)
*   **Rule for Cycle 4:** For bases ending in 2, 3, 7, or 8 (all have cyclicity 4), find `R = Exponent mod 4`.
    *   If `R = 1`, the unit digit is the 1st in the cycle (Base^1 unit digit).
    *   If `R = 2`, the unit digit is the 2nd in the cycle (Base^2 unit digit).
    *   If `R = 3`, the unit digit is the 3rd in the cycle (Base^3 unit digit).
    *   If `R = 0` (i.e., exponent is divisible by 4), the unit digit is the *4th* in the cycle (Base^4 unit digit).
*   **Exponent Mod 4:** To find `Exponent mod 4` quickly, just look at the number formed by the last two digits of the exponent (`453 -> 53`. `53 mod 4 = 1`).
*   **Distractor Check:** (A) 1 is the 4th digit in the cycle (exponent remainder 0). (B) 3 is the 3rd digit (exponent remainder 3). (D) 9 is the 2nd digit (exponent remainder 2). Ensure you correctly map the exponent remainder (1 in this case) to the cycle position.
