# Unit Digit of Sum Product Example

**Problem:**

What is the unit digit of the expression: (257^33) * (346^78) + (129^41)?

(A) 1
(B) 3
(C) 5
(D) 7

## Solution:

This problem requires finding the unit digit of an expression involving multiplication and addition of numbers raised to powers. We need to find the unit digit of each term separately and then apply the rules for unit digits of products and sums, as covered in [[01 - Number System/05 - Units Digit.md]] (Explains finding unit digits of powers and operations).

**Step 1: Find the unit digit of 257^33.**
   - The unit digit of the base is 7.
   - Determine the cyclicity of 7: (7, 9, 3, 1). Cycle length is 4.
   - Find the remainder of the exponent 33 when divided by the cycle length 4: `33 mod 4 = 1`.
   - A remainder of 1 corresponds to the 1st unit digit in the cycle, which is 7.
   - So, the unit digit of 257^33 is 7.

**Step 2: Find the unit digit of 346^78.**
   - The unit digit of the base is 6.
   - The unit digit of any positive integer power of 6 is always 6 (Cyclicity 1: (6)).
   - So, the unit digit of 346^78 is 6.

**Step 3: Find the unit digit of the product (257^33) * (346^78).**
   - Unit digit = Unit digit of (Unit digit of 257^33 * Unit digit of 346^78)
   - Unit digit = Unit digit of (7 * 6)
   - Unit digit = Unit digit of 42
   - Unit digit = 2.

**Step 4: Find the unit digit of 129^41.**
   - The unit digit of the base is 9.
   - Determine the cyclicity of 9: (9, 1). Cycle length is 2.
   - Find the remainder of the exponent 41 when divided by the cycle length 2: `41 mod 2 = 1` (since 41 is odd).
   - A remainder of 1 corresponds to the 1st unit digit in the cycle, which is 9.
   - So, the unit digit of 129^41 is 9.

**Step 5: Find the unit digit of the sum (Product from Step 3) + (Term from Step 4).**
   - Unit digit = Unit digit of (Unit digit of Product + Unit digit of 129^41)
   - Unit digit = Unit digit of (2 + 9)
   - Unit digit = Unit digit of 11
   - Unit digit = 1.

Therefore, the unit digit of the expression (257^33) * (346^78) + (129^41) is 1.

This matches option (A).

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Break Down the Problem:** Address each power term individually first.
*   **Unit Digits Only:** From the start, only consider the unit digits of the bases (7, 6, 9). Ignore the other digits (25, 34, 12).
*   **Apply Rules for Operations:** Remember that the unit digit of a sum/product depends only on the unit digits of the numbers being added/multiplied.
    *   Unit Digit (A * B) = Unit Digit (UnitDigit(A) * UnitDigit(B))
    *   Unit Digit (A + B) = Unit Digit (UnitDigit(A) + UnitDigit(B))
*   **Master Cyclicity Rules:** Knowing the cycle lengths (1 for 0,1,5,6; 2 for 4,9; 4 for 2,3,7,8) and the patterns saves time. [[01 - Number System/05.E01 - Unit Digit of Large Power (Cycle 4) Example]] and [[01 - Number System/05.E02 - Unit Digit of Large Power (Cycle 2) Example]] show examples for different cycle lengths.
*   **Modular Arithmetic:** Finding the unit digit is essentially finding the number `mod 10`. All calculations can be thought of in terms of `mod 10`.
*   **Distractor Check:** Option (B) 3 might arise from 7 * 6 = 42 -> 2; 2 + 9 = 11 -> 1. Error could be 7-6+9=10->0 or 7+6+9=22->2. Option (C) 5 is unlikely here. Option (D) 7 is the unit digit of the first term. Ensure you perform all operations correctly.
