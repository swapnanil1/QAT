# LCM using Prime Factorization Example

**Problem:** Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of 24, 36, and 40.

(A) 120
(B) 240
(C) 360
(D) 720

## Solution:

We will find the LCM using the prime factorization method, as outlined in the parent concept [[01 - Number System/03 - HCF and LCM.md]] (Describes finding LCM by taking the highest powers of all prime factors).

Step 1: Find the prime factorization of each number.
   - 24 = 8 * 3 = 2^3 * 3^1
   - 36 = 4 * 9 = 2^2 * 3^2
   - 40 = 8 * 5 = 2^3 * 5^1

Step 2: Identify all distinct prime factors present in any of the factorizations.
   The distinct prime factors are 2, 3, and 5.

Step 3: For each distinct prime factor, find the highest power that appears in any of the factorizations.
   - Highest power of 2: 2^3 (from 24 and 40)
   - Highest power of 3: 3^2 (from 36)
   - Highest power of 5: 5^1 (from 40)

Step 4: The LCM is the product of these highest powers.
   LCM = 2^3 * 3^2 * 5^1
   LCM = 8 * 9 * 5
   LCM = 72 * 5
   LCM = 360

This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Organized Factorization:** List the prime factorizations vertically, aligning common primes, to easily spot the highest powers:
    ```
    24 = 2^3 * 3^1
    36 = 2^2 * 3^2
    40 = 2^3       * 5^1
    --------------------
    LCM= 2^3 * 3^2 * 5^1
    ```
*   **Highest Power Rule:** Ensure you take the *highest* power for each prime found across *all* numbers. A common mistake is taking the lowest power (used for HCF) or missing a prime factor that appears in only one number (like 5 here).
*   **Alternative: Division Method:** For finding LCM, the common division method ([[01 - Number System/03.E04 - LCM using Common Division Method Example.md]] (Illustrates the division method)) can sometimes be faster, especially if you spot common factors easily.
*   **Distractor Check:**
    *   (A) 120: This is the LCM of 24 and 40 (2^3 * 3^1 * 5^1 = 8*3*5 = 120) but doesn't account for 36 (needs 3^2). It's also 24 * 5.
    *   (B) 240: This is the LCM of 24, 40, and maybe 60, but not 36. 240 = 2^4 * 3 * 5. Incorrect powers.
    *   (D) 720: This is 2 * 360. It is *a* common multiple, but not the *least* common multiple. Double-checking the highest powers prevents this error. It's also the LCM of 36 and 40 (2^3 * 3^2 * 5^1 = 8 * 9 * 5 = 360) and 24. Wait, 720 = 2 * 360. Ah, 720 = 2^4 * 3^2 * 5^1. This would be the LCM if one of the numbers had 2^4. Distractor could arise from multiplying all factors without selecting highest powers or simply doubling the correct LCM. Let's re-check calculation: 2^3 * 3^2 * 5^1 = 8 * 9 * 5 = 72 * 5 = 360. Yes, 360 is correct.
