# HCF using Euclidean Algorithm Example

**Problem:** Find the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of 437 and 667.

(A) 1
(B) 19
(C) 23
(D) 437

## Solution:

We need to find the HCF of 437 and 667. Since the numbers are relatively large and their prime factors are not immediately obvious, the Euclidean Algorithm, as described in [[01 - Number System/03 - HCF and LCM.md]] (Details the step-by-step process of the algorithm), is an efficient method.

Step 1: Divide the larger number (667) by the smaller number (437) and find the remainder.
   667 = 1 * 437 + 230

Step 2: Since the remainder (230) is not 0, replace the larger number with the smaller number (437) and the smaller number with the remainder (230). Divide 437 by 230.
   437 = 1 * 230 + 207

Step 3: The remainder (207) is not 0. Repeat the process: divide 230 by 207.
   230 = 1 * 207 + 23

Step 4: The remainder (23) is not 0. Repeat the process: divide 207 by 23.
   207 = 9 * 23 + 0

Step 5: The remainder is now 0. The HCF is the last non-zero remainder, which was the divisor in the last step where the remainder became 0.
   The last non-zero remainder is 23.

Therefore, the HCF of 437 and 667 is 23.

This matches option (C).

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Efficiency:** The Euclidean Algorithm is significantly faster than prime factorization for large numbers or numbers whose prime factors are hard to find quickly (like 437 = 19 * 23 and 667 = 23 * 29).
*   **Core Principle:** Remember the key step: `HCF(a, b) = HCF(b, a % b)`, where `a % b` is the remainder when `a` is divided by `b`. Keep applying this until the remainder is 0.
*   **Last Divisor:** The HCF is the *last non-zero remainder*, which is equivalent to the *divisor* in the step where the remainder becomes 0. Don't mistakenly take the final quotient (9 in this case) as the HCF.
*   **Distractor Check:** Option (A) 1 would be the HCF if the numbers were co-prime. Option (B) 19 is a factor of 437, but not 667. Option (D) 437 is one of the original numbers, which can only be the HCF if it divides the other number exactly (which it doesn't here). Intermediate remainders (like 230, 207) are not the HCF.
