# Cricket Average Runs Example

**Problem:**

A cricketer had a certain average of runs for his 10 innings. In his 11th innings, he scored 64 runs, and thereby his average of runs increased by 2. What was his average after the 11th innings?

(A) 40
(B) 42
(C) 44
(D) 46

## Solution:

This problem involves calculating the average score based on the change caused by a specific innings score. We use concepts from [[08 - Averages/04 - Problems on Ages, Runs, etc.md]].

**Method 1: Using Algebra and Total Sum**

1.  **Let the average after 10 innings be `A`.**
    Total runs after 10 innings = `10 * A`.

2.  **After the 11th innings:**
    Number of innings = 11.
    Runs scored in the 11th innings = 64.
    Total runs after 11 innings = Total runs after 10 innings + Runs in 11th innings
    Total runs after 11 innings = `10A + 64`.

3.  **The new average after 11 innings is `A + 2` (original average increased by 2).**
    We also know that:
    Total runs after 11 innings = New Average * New Number of Innings
    Total runs after 11 innings = `(A + 2) * 11`.

4.  **Equate the two expressions for the total runs after 11 innings:**
    `10A + 64 = (A + 2) * 11`
    `10A + 64 = 11A + 22`

5.  **Solve for `A` (the average after 10 innings):**
    Subtract `10A` from both sides: `64 = A + 22`
    Subtract 22 from both sides: `A = 64 - 22`
    `A = 42`.
    So, the average after 10 innings was 42.

6.  **Calculate the average after the 11th innings:**
    New Average = `A + 2`
    New Average = 42 + 2 = 44.

The average after the 11th innings is 44. This matches option (C).

**Method 2: Using the Effect on Average (Conceptual Shortcut)**

1.  The batsman scored 64 runs in the 11th innings.
2.  This score increased the average by 2 runs over 11 innings.
3.  The *total extra runs* scored in the 11th innings *above* what would have maintained the *old* average is responsible for increasing the average of all 11 innings by 2.
    Total increase contributed = Increase in Average * Number of Innings = 2 * 11 = 22 runs.
4.  This means the 64 runs scored consist of two parts: (a) runs needed to match the *old* average, and (b) the extra 22 runs that increased the average.
    Runs Scored = Old Average + Total Increase Contributed
    `64 = Old Average + 22`
5.  Solve for the Old Average (average after 10 innings):
    Old Average = 64 - 22 = 42.
6.  The new average (after 11 innings) is the old average plus the increase:
    New Average = Old Average + 2 = 42 + 2 = 44.

## Shortcut/Tip

Method 2 provides a quicker logical path often suitable for MCQs:

1.  **Calculate the 'total effect'** of the last innings on the sum: Change in Average * Final Number of Innings = `2 * 11 = 22`. This positive value represents the surplus runs scored in the last innings compared to the *old* average.
2.  **Relate to the score:** Score in Last Innings = Old Average + Total Effect
    `64 = Old Average + 22`
3.  **Find the Old Average:** Old Average = 64 - 22 = 42.
4.  **Find the New Average:** New Average = Old Average + Change in Average = 42 + 2 = 44.

*Alternative Shortcut using New Average:*
Let the *new* average (after 11 innings) be `A_new`.
The score in the 11th innings (64) must be equal to the new average plus the amount needed to 'lift' the previous 10 innings' average up to the new average.
Score = `A_new + (Number of Previous Innings * Increase in Average)`
`64 = A_new + (10 * 2)`
`64 = A_new + 20`
`A_new = 64 - 20 = 44`. This directly finds the new average.
