# 08 - Averages/04 - Problems on Ages, Runs, etc

This section focuses on applying the fundamental concepts of averages ([[08 - Averages/01 - Basic Average Formula.md]]) and their properties ([[08 - Averages/02 - Properties of Averages.md]]) to specific types of quantitative aptitude problems, most commonly involving:

1.  **Ages:** Problems dealing with the average age of a group of people, how it changes over time, or when individuals join or leave the group.
2.  **Cricket Runs (or Scores):** Problems involving a batsman's average score over a number of innings, how it changes after a particular innings, or calculating runs needed to achieve a certain average. Similar logic applies to average marks, temperatures, etc.

**Key Strategies and Concepts Used:**

1.  **Sum = Average × Number:** The relationship `Sum = Average * Number of Observations` is central to solving these problems.
    *   For ages: `Total Age = Average Age * Number of People`
    *   For runs: `Total Runs = Average Runs * Number of Innings`

2.  **Calculating Changes:** Problems often involve finding a missing value (like the age of a new person or runs in the last innings) based on how the average changes.
    *   **Step 1:** Calculate the initial total sum (age/runs) before the change.
    *   **Step 2:** Calculate the final total sum (age/runs) after the change.
    *   **Step 3:** The difference between the final and initial sum gives the value of the item added/removed.
    *   **Example:** [[08 - Averages/02.E01 - Effect of Adding Removing Value Example.md]] showed how to find the teacher's age when added to a class.

3.  **Shortcut using Change in Average:** A faster method focuses on the change in the average itself.
    *   When a person/innings is added:
        `Value Added = Old Average + (Change in Average * New Number of Observations)`
    *   When a person/innings is removed:
        `Value Removed = Old Average - (Change in Average * Old Number of Observations)`
        *(Note: The 'Change in Average' here refers to the difference between the old and new average; be mindful of signs.)*
    *   This shortcut leverages the properties discussed in [[08 - Averages/02 - Properties of Averages.md]].

**Common Scenarios:**

*   **Age Problems:**
    *   Finding the age of a new person joining a group.
    *   Finding the age of a person leaving a group.
    *   Finding the age of a person replacing another.
    *   Calculating the average age of a group 'n' years ago or 'n' years later (remembering that *each* person's age changes by 'n').
    *   **Example:** [[08 - Averages/04.E01 - Average Age Problems Example.md]]

*   **Runs/Score Problems:**
    *   Calculating runs scored in the last innings to achieve a certain average.
    *   Calculating the average after a certain number of innings given the total runs or vice-versa.
    *   Finding the effect of a very high or low score (like 0) on the average.
    *   **Example:** [[08 - Averages/04.E02 - Cricket Average Runs Example.md]]

**General Tips:**

*   Read the question carefully to understand whether a value is being added, removed, or replaced.
*   Keep track of the "Number of Observations" (people, innings) before and after the change.
*   Use the `Sum = Average * Number` relationship consistently.
*   Consider using the shortcut method focusing on the change in average for faster calculations in MCQs.
