# Weighted Average Calculation Example

**Problem:**

A school has three sections for Class X. Section A has 30 students with an average score of 75 in a math test. Section B has 40 students with an average score of 80. Section C has 20 students with an average score of 70. What is the overall average score for Class X in the math test?

(A) 75.0
(B) 76 1/9
(C) 76.5
(D) 77.0

## Solution:

This problem requires calculating the weighted average, where the average scores of the sections are the 'values' and the number of students in each section are the 'weights'. We use the weighted average formula from [[08 - Averages/03 - Weighted Average.md]]:

`Aw = (w1*x1 + w2*x2 + w3*x3) / (w1 + w2 + w3)`

Here:
*   Section A: `x1 = 75` (average score), `w1 = 30` (number of students)
*   Section B: `x2 = 80` (average score), `w2 = 40` (number of students)
*   Section C: `x3 = 70` (average score), `w3 = 20` (number of students)

**Step 1: Calculate the sum of the products of weights and values (`Σ(wi * xi)`).**
`w1*x1 = 30 * 75 = 2250`
`w2*x2 = 40 * 80 = 3200`
`w3*x3 = 20 * 70 = 1400`

Sum = `2250 + 3200 + 1400 = 6850`

**Step 2: Calculate the sum of the weights (`Σwi`).**
Sum of weights = `w1 + w2 + w3 = 30 + 40 + 20 = 90`

**Step 3: Calculate the weighted average.**
Weighted Average `Aw = (Sum of products) / (Sum of weights)`
`Aw = 6850 / 90`
`Aw = 685 / 9`

To convert this to a mixed fraction:
`685 ÷ 9`:
`9 * 7 = 63` -> Remainder `68 - 63 = 5`. Bring down 5. -> 55.
`9 * 6 = 54` -> Remainder `55 - 54 = 1`.
So, `685 / 9 = 76` with a remainder of `1`.
`Aw = 76 1/9`

The overall average score for Class X is 76 1/9. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip

1.  **Identify Values and Weights:** Clearly distinguish between the quantities being averaged (scores: 75, 80, 70) and their corresponding weights (number of students: 30, 40, 20).
2.  **Avoid Simple Average:** Do *not* calculate `(75 + 80 + 70) / 3 = 75`. This ignores the fact that Section B has more influence due to more students. The weighted average must be used.
3.  **Estimation Check:** The weighted average must lie between the minimum (70) and maximum (80) individual averages. Since the largest group (Section B) has the highest average (80), the overall average should be pulled towards 80, i.e., it should be greater than the simple average of 75. This helps eliminate option (A) quickly.
4.  **Simplify Weights (Optional):** If the weights (30, 40, 20) have a common factor, you can simplify them *before* calculation. Here, the common factor is 10. Simplified weights: 3, 4, 2.
    `Aw = (3*75 + 4*80 + 2*70) / (3 + 4 + 2)`
    `Aw = (225 + 320 + 140) / 9`
    `Aw = 685 / 9 = 76 1/9`. This reduces the size of the numbers in the calculation.
