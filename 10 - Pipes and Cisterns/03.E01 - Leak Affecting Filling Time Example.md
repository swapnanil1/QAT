# Leak Affecting Filling Time Example

**Problem:**

An electric pump can fill a tank in 3 hours. However, due to a leak in the tank, it takes 3.5 hours (or 7/2 hours) to fill the tank. If the tank is full, how much time will the leak take to empty it?

(A) 21 hours
(B) 20 hours
(C) 7 hours
(D) 10.5 hours

## Solution:

This problem asks for the time the leak takes to empty the tank alone, given the pump's filling time and the combined filling time with the leak active. We use the LCM method as described in [[10 - Pipes and Cisterns/03 - Tank Leakage Problems.md]].

Let the Pump be P (inlet) and the Leak be L (outlet).

**Step 1: Assume Tank Capacity using LCM**
Time taken by Pump P alone to fill (`T_P`) = 3 hours.
Time taken by Pump P and Leak L together to fill (`T_P+L`) = 3.5 hours = 7/2 hours.

To find the LCM of 3 and 7/2, we find the LCM of the numerators (3, 7) and the HCF of the denominators (1, 2).
LCM(3, 7) = 21.
HCF(1, 2) = 1.
LCM(3, 7/2) = LCM(Numerators) / HCF(Denominators) = 21 / 1 = 21.
Let the Total Capacity of the tank = 21 units.

**Step 2: Calculate Efficiencies**
Efficiency = Capacity / Time Taken

*   Efficiency of Pump P (`Eff_P`) = 21 units / 3 hours = +7 units/hour (Positive as it fills).
*   Combined Efficiency of Pump P and Leak L (`Eff_P+L`) = 21 units / (7/2 hours) = 21 * (2/7) units/hour = 3 * 2 = +6 units/hour (Positive as the tank still fills).

**Step 3: Calculate the Efficiency of the Leak (L)**
The combined efficiency is the algebraic sum:
`Eff_P+L = Eff_P + Eff_L`
`+6 = (+7) + Eff_L`

Solve for `Eff_L`:
`Eff_L = +6 - (+7)`
`Eff_L = 6 - 7 = -1` unit/hour.

The efficiency of the leak is -1 unit/hour, indicating it empties 1 unit per hour.

**Step 4: Calculate Time Taken by the Leak Alone to Empty**
Time for Leak L to empty = Total Capacity / |Efficiency of L|
Time for Leak L to empty = 21 units / |-1 unit/hour|
Time for Leak L to empty = 21 / 1 = 21 hours.

The leak alone will take 21 hours to empty the full tank. This matches option (A).

## Shortcut/Tip

Similar to [[10 - Pipes and Cisterns/01.E02 - One Filling, One Emptying Pipe Example.md]], we can use the formula derived from the fraction method for finding the outlet's time (`y`) when the inlet's time (`x`) and the combined filling time (`t`) are known:

`y = xt / (t - x)`

Here:
`x = 3` hours (Pump's time)
`t = 3.5` hours (Combined filling time)
`y = ?` (Leak's emptying time)

`y = (3 * 3.5) / (3.5 - 3)`
`y = 10.5 / 0.5`
`y = 105 / 5`
`y = 21` hours.

This formula provides a direct answer. Ensure `t > x` when using this specific formula version (as the combined time must be longer than the inlet time alone if there's a leak).
