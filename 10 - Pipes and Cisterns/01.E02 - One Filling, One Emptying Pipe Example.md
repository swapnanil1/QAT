# One Filling, One Emptying Pipe Example

**Problem:**

A pipe can fill a tank in 10 hours. Due to a leak at the bottom, it takes 15 hours to fill the tank when both the pipe and the leak are active. In how many hours can the leak alone empty the full tank?

(A) 12 hours
(B) 18 hours
(C) 25 hours
(D) 30 hours

## Solution:

This problem involves an inlet pipe working against an outlet (leak). We can use the LCM method, treating the leak as doing negative work, based on concepts from [[10 - Pipes and Cisterns/01 - Basic Concepts (Filling Emptying Rates, LCM Method).md]].

Let Pipe A be the inlet pipe and Pipe B be the leak (outlet).

**Step 1: Assume Tank Capacity using LCM**
Time taken by Pipe A alone to fill = 10 hours.
Time taken by Pipe A and Leak B together to fill = 15 hours.
LCM(10, 15) = 30.
Let the Total Capacity of the tank = 30 units.

**Step 2: Calculate Efficiencies**
Efficiency = Capacity / Time Taken

*   Efficiency of Pipe A (`Eff_A`) = 30 units / 10 hours = +3 units/hour (Positive as it fills).
*   Combined Efficiency of Pipe A and Leak B (`Eff_A+B`) = 30 units / 15 hours = +2 units/hour (Positive as the tank still fills, albeit slower).

**Step 3: Calculate the Efficiency of the Leak (B)**
The combined efficiency is the algebraic sum of individual efficiencies:
`Eff_A+B = Eff_A + Eff_B`
Here, `Eff_B` represents the efficiency of the leak, which should be negative.
`+2 = (+3) + Eff_B`

Solve for `Eff_B`:
`Eff_B = +2 - (+3)`
`Eff_B = 2 - 3 = -1` unit/hour.

The negative sign confirms that B is an outlet/leak, and its efficiency (emptying rate) is 1 unit per hour.

**Step 4: Calculate Time Taken by the Leak Alone to Empty**
Time for Leak B to empty = Total Capacity / |Efficiency of B|
Time for Leak B to empty = 30 units / |-1 unit/hour|
Time for Leak B to empty = 30 / 1 = 30 hours.

So, the leak alone can empty the full tank in 30 hours. This matches option (D).

## Shortcut/Tip

For problems with one inlet (time `x`) and one outlet (time `y`), where the combined operation results in filling (time `t`), the relationship derived from the fraction method is:
`1/x - 1/y = 1/t` (if the outlet's time `y` is what we need to find)
`1/y = 1/x - 1/t`
`1/y = (t - x) / xt`
`y = xt / (t - x)`

Here:
`x = 10` (Inlet time)
`t = 15` (Combined filling time)
`y = ?` (Outlet/Leak time)

`y = (10 * 15) / (15 - 10)`
`y = 150 / 5`
`y = 30` hours.

This formula `y = xt / (t - x)` provides a direct calculation when the combined effect is *filling*. If the combined effect was *emptying* in time `t`, the formula would be `1/y - 1/x = 1/t` leading to `y = xt / (x - t)`. Using the LCM method with signed efficiencies avoids confusion about which formula to use.
