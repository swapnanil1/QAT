# Pipe Closed After Some Time Example

**Problem:**

Two pipes A and B can fill a tank in 10 hours and 15 hours respectively. Both pipes are opened together, but after 4 hours, pipe B is closed. In how many total hours will the tank be filled?

(A) 6 hours
(B) 7 1/3 hours
(C) 3 1/3 hours
(D) 8 hours

## Solution:

This problem requires tracking the work done in phases, as one pipe is closed partway through. We use the LCM method as outlined in [[10 - Pipes and Cisterns/02 - Pipes Opened Closed Intermittently.md]].

**Step 1: Assume Tank Capacity using LCM**
Time taken by Pipe A = 10 hours.
Time taken by Pipe B = 15 hours.
LCM(10, 15) = 30.
Let the Total Capacity of the tank = 30 units.

**Step 2: Calculate Individual Efficiencies**
Efficiency of A (`Eff_A`) = 30 units / 10 hours = +3 units/hour.
Efficiency of B (`Eff_B`) = 30 units / 15 hours = +2 units/hour.

**Step 3: Calculate Work Done in the First Interval (0-4 hours)**
During the first 4 hours, both pipes A and B are open.
Combined Efficiency (`Eff_A+B`) = `Eff_A + Eff_B` = 3 + 2 = 5 units/hour.
Work done in the first 4 hours = Combined Efficiency * Time = 5 units/hour * 4 hours = 20 units.

**Step 4: Calculate Remaining Work**
After 4 hours, 20 units of the tank are filled.
Remaining Work = Total Capacity - Work Done = 30 units - 20 units = 10 units.

**Step 5: Calculate Time Taken for the Second Interval (After 4 hours)**
After 4 hours, Pipe B is closed. Only Pipe A remains open.
Efficiency during the second interval = `Eff_A` = 3 units/hour.
Time required to fill the remaining work = Remaining Work / Efficiency of A
Time for remaining work = 10 units / 3 units/hour = 10/3 hours.

**Step 6: Calculate Total Time**
The total time taken to fill the tank is the sum of the time for the first interval and the time for the second interval.
Total Time = Time (Interval 1) + Time (Interval 2)
Total Time = 4 hours + 10/3 hours
Total Time = 12/3 hours + 10/3 hours
Total Time = 22/3 hours.

Convert 22/3 hours to a mixed fraction:
22 ÷ 3 = 7 with a remainder of 1.
Total Time = 7 1/3 hours.

The tank will be filled in a total of 7 1/3 hours. This matches option (B).

## Shortcut/Tip

1.  **Calculate Initial Phase:** Find the combined efficiency (A+B = 5 units/hr). Calculate work done in the initial period (4 hours * 5 units/hr = 20 units).
2.  **Find Remainder:** Calculate the remaining work (30 - 20 = 10 units).
3.  **Calculate Final Phase Time:** Identify the active pipe(s) (only A) and their efficiency (3 units/hr). Calculate time for the remainder (Remaining Work / Active Efficiency = 10 / 3 hours).
4.  **Add Times:** Sum the time from the initial phase and the final phase (4 + 10/3 = 22/3 = 7 1/3 hours).

A common mistake is to only calculate the time for the remaining work (10/3 hours or 3 1/3 hours - option C) and forget to add the initial 4 hours. Always read the question carefully to see if it asks for the *remaining* time or the *total* time. Another mistake is to assume both pipes run for the whole duration (30 / 5 = 6 hours - option A).
