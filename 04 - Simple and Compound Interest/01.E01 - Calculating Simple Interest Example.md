**Problem:**

A sum of money was invested at simple interest at a certain rate for 2 years. Had it been invested at a rate 3% higher, the interest earned would have been Rs. 72 more. What was the principal amount invested?

**Options:**
(A) Rs. 2400
(B) Rs. 1150
(C) Rs. 1200
(D) Rs. 1250

**Solution:**

This problem tests the understanding of how a change in the rate affects the simple interest when the principal and time are constant.

**1. Identify the variables and conditions:**
*   Let the Principal amount be P.
*   Time (T) = 2 years.
*   Let the original Rate be R% p.a.
*   Let the new Rate be R' = (R + 3)% p.a.
*   Original Simple Interest (SI) = (P * R * 2) / 100
*   New Simple Interest (SI') = (P * (R + 3) * 2) / 100
*   The difference in interest is given: SI' - SI = Rs. 72.

**2. Set up the equation based on the difference:**

SI' - SI = 72
[(P * (R + 3) * 2) / 100] - [(P * R * 2) / 100] = 72

**3. Simplify the equation:**

Factor out common terms (P * 2 / 100):
(P * 2 / 100) * [(R + 3) - R] = 72
(P * 2 / 100) * [3] = 72

**4. Solve for P:**

(P * 6) / 100 = 72
P * 6 = 72 * 100
P * 6 = 7200
P = 7200 / 6
P = 1200

**Alternative (Shortcut) Method:**

*   The *difference* in simple interest is solely due to the interest calculated on the *difference* in the rate (3%) for the given time period (2 years).
*   Extra Interest = (P * Extra Rate * T) / 100
*   Here, Extra Interest = Rs. 72, Extra Rate = 3%, T = 2 years.
*   72 = (P * 3 * 2) / 100
*   72 = (P * 6) / 100
*   P = (72 * 100) / 6
*   P = 12 * 100
*   P = 1200

**Answer:**

The principal amount invested was Rs. 1200.
