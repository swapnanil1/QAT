**Problem:**

Calculate the compound interest on Rs. 8,000 for 1.5 years (1 year and 6 months) at a rate of 10% per annum, compounded half-yearly.
**Options:**
(A) Rs. 1200
(B) Rs. 2648
(C) Rs. 1240
(D) Rs. 1261

**Solution:**

This problem requires adjusting the rate and time period according to the half-yearly compounding frequency.

**1. Identify the variables and adjust for half-yearly compounding:**
*   Principal (P) = Rs. 8,000
*   Annual Rate (R) = 10% p.a.
*   Time (T) = 1.5 years
*   Compounding is half-yearly (k=2).

*   **Adjusted Rate (r):** The rate needs to be applied per compounding period (half-year).
    Rate per half-year = Annual Rate / k = 10% / 2 = 5% per half-year.
    For formula use, the rate fraction `r = (R/k)/100` which equals 5/100 here.

*   **Adjusted Time (n):** The time needs to be expressed as the total number of compounding periods.
    Number of compounding periods `n = T * k` = 1.5 * 2 = 3 periods (half-years).

**2. Apply the Compound Amount formula:**

The general formula for amount (A) is `A = P * (1 + (R/k)/100)^(k*T)`.
Using the adjusted rate per period (`r = 5/100`) and number of periods (n=3), the formula becomes:
`A = P * (1 + r)^n`
Substituting the value of r:
`A = P * (1 + 5/100)^n`
`A = P * (1 + 1/20)^n`
`A = P * (21/20)^n`

**3. Substitute the Principal (P) and Number of periods (n) and calculate Amount (A):**

Substituting P = 8000 and n = 3 into `A = P * (21/20)^n`:
A = 8000 * (21/20)^3
A = 8000 * (21/20) * (21/20) * (21/20)
A = 8000 * (9261 / 8000)
A = 9261

So, the Amount after 1.5 years is Rs. 9,261.

**4. Calculate the Compound Interest (CI):**

`CI = A - P`
CI = 9261 - 8000
CI = Rs. 1,261

**Key Takeaway:**

When compounding is not annual, **always** adjust the rate (divide by k to get `r`) and time (multiply by k to get `n`) *before* plugging them into the core Amount formula `A = P*(1+r)^n`. Misinterpreting R and T in the context of compounding frequency is a common error.

**Answer:**

The compound interest is Rs. 1,261.

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Fraction Method:** Using the factor `(1 + r)` as a fraction (21/20 here) can simplify calculations, especially when the Principal has factors that cancel with the denominator raised to power `n` (e.g., `8000` cancels with `20^3 = 8000`).
*   **Mental Calculation (Approximation):** Treat as 3 periods at 5%.
    *   Period 1 Interest: 5% of 8000 = 400. Amount = 8400.
    *   Period 2 Interest: 5% of 8400 = 420. Amount = 8820.
    *   Period 3 Interest: 5% of 8820 = 441. Amount = 9261.
    *   Total CI = 400 + 420 + 441 = 1261. This confirms the result and helps understand the compounding effect.
*   **Avoid SI Calculation:** Do not calculate SI for 1.5 years (which would be `(8000 * 10 * 1.5) / 100 = 1200`). CI will always be slightly higher than SI (for T > 1 period), so SI provides a lower bound for MCQ elimination.
