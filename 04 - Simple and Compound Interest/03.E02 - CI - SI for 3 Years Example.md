**Problem:**

The difference between the compound interest and simple interest on a certain sum for 3 years at 10% per annum is Rs. 93. Find the sum (Principal).

**Options:**
(A) Rs. 2500
(B) Rs. 3500
(C) Rs. 3000
(D) Rs. 4000

**Solution:**

This problem requires the application of the specific formula for the difference between CI and SI for a period of 3 years.

**1. Identify the given information:**
*   Difference (CI - SI) = Rs. 93
*   Rate (R) = 10% p.a.
*   Time (T) = 3 years
*   Principal (P) = ? (This needs to be found)

**2. Recall the formula for the difference for 3 years:**

The difference between CI and SI for 3 years (compounded annually) is given by:
`CI - SI (for 3 years) = P * (R / 100)^2 * (3 + R / 100)`

**3. Substitute the known values into the formula:**

93 = P * (10 / 100)^2 * (3 + 10 / 100)
93 = P * (1 / 10)^2 * (3 + 1 / 10)
93 = P * (1 / 100) * (30/10 + 1/10)
93 = P * (1 / 100) * (31 / 10)
93 = P * (31 / 1000)

**4. Solve for P:**

Multiply both sides by 1000 and divide by 31:
P = (93 * 1000) / 31
Notice that 93 = 3 * 31.
P = (3 * 31 * 1000) / 31
P = 3 * 1000
P = 3000

**Answer:**

The sum (Principal) is Rs. 3000.
Correct Option: **(C)**

## Shortcut/Tip

*   **Direct Formula:** Memorizing the formula `Diff(3 yrs) = P * (R/100)^2 * (3 + R/100)` is crucial for solving these questions quickly in exams. Calculating CI and SI separately for 3 years and then finding the difference is very time-consuming.
*   **Structure Recognition:** Notice the formula contains the term for the 2-year difference `P*(R/100)^2`. The 3-year difference is essentially this term multiplied by `(3 + R/100)`.
*   **Simplification:** Simplify `R/100` to its fraction form early (10/100 = 1/10). This makes the calculation `(3 + R/100)` easier (3 + 1/10 = 31/10).
*   **Check Divisibility:** In the step `93 = P * (31 / 1000)`, notice that 93 is divisible by 31 (`93/31 = 3`). This often happens in well-constructed aptitude questions and speeds up finding P (`P = 3 * 1000 = 3000`). If 93 wasn't divisible by 31, you'd suspect a calculation error or the question might involve approximation.
