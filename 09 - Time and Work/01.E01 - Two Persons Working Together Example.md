# Two Persons Working Together Example

**Problem:**

A can complete a piece of work in 18 days. B is 50% more efficient than A. In how many days can A and B, working together, complete the same piece of work?

(A) 7 1/5 days
(B) 9 days
(C) 6 days
(D) 8 days

## Solution:

This problem combines the concept of efficiency comparison with finding the time taken when working together. We will use the LCM method / Efficiency Method described in [[09 - Time and Work/01 - Basic Concepts (Work Rate, LCM Method).md]].

**Step 1: Define Efficiency Relationship**
B is 50% more efficient than A. This means if A does `x` units of work per day, B does `x + 0.50x = 1.5x` units per day.
The ratio of their efficiencies (A : B) is `x : 1.5x` which simplifies to `1 : 1.5` or `2 : 3`.
So, B's efficiency = (3/2) * A's efficiency.

**Step 2: Assume Total Work and Calculate Individual Efficiencies**
A completes the work in 18 days. Let's assume the Total Work is the LCM of the time periods involved. However, we only have A's time. A convenient way is to assume Total Work based on A's time and derive B's time/efficiency.

Let A's efficiency (work per day) be `Eff_A`.
Let B's efficiency (work per day) be `Eff_B`.
From Step 1, `Eff_B = (3/2) * Eff_A`.

We know `Time = Total Work / Efficiency`.
For A: `18 = Total Work / Eff_A`.
Let's assume `Eff_A = 2` units/day (choosing 2 makes `Eff_B` an integer).
Then `Eff_B = (3/2) * 2 = 3` units/day.

Now, find the Total Work using A's data:
Total Work = `Time_A * Eff_A` = 18 days * 2 units/day = 36 units.

*(Alternative way to set Total Work: Assume Total Work = 18 units based on A's time. Then A's efficiency = 18/18 = 1 unit/day. B's efficiency = 1.5 * 1 = 1.5 units/day. This also works but involves decimals/fractions.)*

Using Total Work = 36 units:
A's efficiency = 36 / 18 = 2 units/day.
B's efficiency = (3/2) * A's efficiency = (3/2) * 2 = 3 units/day.

**Step 3: Calculate Combined Efficiency**
When A and B work together, their combined efficiency is the sum of their individual efficiencies.
Combined Efficiency (`Eff_A+B`) = `Eff_A + Eff_B` = 2 units/day + 3 units/day = 5 units/day.

**Step 4: Calculate Time Taken Together**
Time taken together = `Total Work / Combined Efficiency`
Time taken together = 36 units / 5 units/day = 36/5 days.

Convert 36/5 to a mixed fraction:
36 ÷ 5 = 7 with a remainder of 1.
So, 36/5 days = 7 1/5 days.

A and B working together can complete the work in 7 1/5 days. This matches option (A).

## Shortcut/Tip

1.  **Ratio of Efficiencies:** Start with the efficiency ratio. B is 50% more efficient than A => Eff(A) : Eff(B) = 100 : 150 = 2 : 3.
2.  **Ratio of Times:** Since Time is inversely proportional to Efficiency, the ratio of time taken is Time(A) : Time(B) = 3 : 2.
3.  **Calculate B's Time:** We know Time(A) = 18 days. If the ratio is 3:2, let the times be `3k` and `2k`. `3k = 18` => `k = 6`. So, Time(B) = `2k = 2 * 6 = 12` days.
4.  **Use the Combined Work Formula (for two people):** Time(A+B) = `(Time(A) * Time(B)) / (Time(A) + Time(B))`
    Time(A+B) = `(18 * 12) / (18 + 12)`
    Time(A+B) = `(18 * 12) / 30`
    Time(A+B) = `(3 * 12) / 5` (dividing 18 and 30 by 6)
    Time(A+B) = `36 / 5` = 7 1/5 days.

This shortcut quickly finds B's individual time first, then applies the standard formula for two workers, avoiding the need to explicitly calculate LCM or work units.
